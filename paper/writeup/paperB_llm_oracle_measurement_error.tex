\begin{abstract}
Large language models are increasingly used as cheap ``oracles''---relevance judges, correctness verifiers,
data annotators---and their outputs are almost always treated as ground truth or, at best, as exchangeably
noisy labels. We argue this is a measurement problem in disguise, and a badly-specified one: LLM judgments
carry \emph{systematic, structured} bias, not random noise. In multi-hop relevance judging we document a
concrete instance---\emph{bridge-blindness}, in which a passage essential to a reasoning chain is judged
irrelevant \emph{because} it does not directly answer the question---and we show the bias is a function of a
latent passage \emph{role}, violating the conditional-random-error assumption of classical rater models
(Dawid--Skene). We develop a Bayesian measurement-error model in which the ordinal LLM judgment is a noisy view
of a latent role with a role-dependent confusion law, and---the central methodological contribution---we use
the \emph{corpus graph} to identify the model \emph{without} gold labels or repeated annotation: relevance is
smooth on the graph, so a confidently-relevant anchor and its neighbours provide correlated latent roles viewed
through the same instrument, separating the judge's bias from the truth. We state identifiability conditions,
give an inference procedure, and target calibrated relevance posteriors whose correction demonstrably improves a
downstream decision. The application is passage retrieval, but the object---rigorous inference under a biased
LLM oracle---recurs across weak supervision, LLM annotation pipelines, and automated evaluation.
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}
An LLM relevance judge returns, for a query $q$ and passage $d_i$, an ordinal score $g_i\in\{0,1,2\}$. Pipelines
consume $g_i$ as truth (thresholding) or aggregate several judges as if errors were random. Neither is right.
We find that a hop-aware judge, however well prompted, systematically under-rates \emph{bridge} passages---those
that supply a linking entity rather than the final answer---while rating \emph{direct-evidence} passages well.
The error is a function of a latent \emph{role}, so it is neither random nor removable by more of the same judge.

This is a measurement-error problem with two nonstandard features: (i) the bias is \emph{structured by a latent
categorical role}, and (ii) we typically have \emph{one} judge and \emph{no} gold, so classical identification
(replication, a gold subset) is unavailable. Our contribution is a model for (i) and a use of corpus structure
to solve (ii). The result is a calibrated posterior over true relevance from biased judgments, and a general
template: \emph{how a statistician should use an LLM oracle}.

\paragraph{Relation to the companion paper.} The companion CS/ML paper uses the corpus graph as the
\emph{covariance} of a latent relevance field (structure propagates \emph{belief}). Here the same graph is the
\emph{instrument that identifies the measurement model} (structure propagates \emph{calibration}). The duality
---one object, two epistemic roles---is the spine of the research program; the two papers are disjoint in
contribution (a retrieval method vs.\ a measurement model) and cite each other.

\section{The Structured Measurement-Error Model}\label{sec:model}
\paragraph{Latent roles.} Each candidate $d_i$ (for a fixed query) has a latent role
\begin{equation}
  z_i \in \{\,\textsf{irrelevant}=0,\ \textsf{bridge}=1,\ \textsf{direct}=2\,\},
\end{equation}
with true relevance $r_i = \mathbf 1\{z_i\ge 1\}$. Bridge and direct passages are both relevant but play
different functional roles in the chain; this distinction is exactly what a naive judge conflates.

\paragraph{Observation law (role-dependent confusion).} A judge (rater) $\rho$ returns
\begin{equation}\label{eq:obs}
  g_i^{(\rho)} \,\big|\, z_i=c \;\sim\; \mathrm{Categorical}\!\big(\pi^{(\rho)}_{c}\big),
  \qquad \pi^{(\rho)}_c \in \Delta^{2},
\end{equation}
a $3\times3$ confusion $\Pi^{(\rho)}=(\pi^{(\rho)}_0,\pi^{(\rho)}_1,\pi^{(\rho)}_2)^\top$. \emph{Bridge-blindness}
is the structural constraint $\pi^{(\rho)}_{1}$ places substantial mass on $g=0$: $\Pr(g{=}0\mid z{=}1)\gg
\Pr(g{=}0\mid z{=}2)$. We parameterize $\Pi$ so this asymmetry is a free but regularized parameter (an ordinal
probit with role-shifted thresholds is an equivalent, more parsimonious form):
\begin{equation}\label{eq:probit}
  g_i^{(\rho)} = \textstyle\sum_{t} \mathbf 1\{\,\eta^{(\rho)}_i > \kappa^{(\rho)}_t\,\},\qquad
  \eta^{(\rho)}_i = \alpha^{(\rho)}_{z_i} + \varepsilon_i,\ \ \varepsilon_i\sim N(0,1),
\end{equation}
with role effects $\alpha^{(\rho)}_c$ (bridge-blindness $\Leftrightarrow \alpha^{(\rho)}_1$ depressed toward
$\alpha^{(\rho)}_0$). \TESTED{The role dependence is real: on our data the judge's recall on \emph{gold} passages
that are semantically buried/graph-bridging is far below its recall on directly-relevant golds, and overall
gold-recall varies $0.57$--$0.89$ across corpora with the fraction of bridge-type evidence.}

\paragraph{Prior on latent roles via corpus structure.} We tie $z$ to (a) the dense retriever and (b) the
corpus graph $A$:
\begin{equation}\label{eq:prior}
  \Pr(z) \;\propto\; \exp\!\Big(\underbrace{\textstyle\sum_i \psi(z_i;\, m_i)}_{\text{semantic anchor}}
  \;+\; \underbrace{\theta \textstyle\sum_{(i,j)\in A} \omega(z_i,z_j)}_{\text{graph smoothness (Potts/GMRF)}}\Big),
\end{equation}
where $\psi$ pulls high-cosine passages toward relevance and $\omega$ rewards graph-connected passages taking
\emph{compatible} roles (a chain is a connected \textsf{direct}--\textsf{bridge} structure). This is the
identifying prior: it says the truth is graph-smooth even though the \emph{judge} is graph-blind.

\section{Identification by Structure (the crux)}\label{sec:ident}
With one rater and no gold, $(\Pi,z)$ are not identified from $\{g_i\}$ alone---any confusion can be absorbed
into a relabeling of $z$. Classical fixes (replication across conditionally-independent raters; a labeled anchor
set) are the two levers; we add a third.

\begin{assumption}[Anchors]\label{as:anchor}
A subset $\mathcal A$ of passages have $\Pr(z_i\ge 1)\to 1$ under the semantic prior (top-cosine passages that
are almost surely relevant), providing partially-known latent roles.
\end{assumption}
\begin{assumption}[Graph-smooth truth, judge-blind]\label{as:smooth}
$z$ follows the Potts prior \eqref{eq:prior} with $\theta>0$, while the confusion $\Pi$ is independent of the
graph given $z$ (the instrument does not ``know'' the structure).
\end{assumption}
\begin{assumption}[Rater or prompt diversity]\label{as:raters}
Optionally, $R\ge 2$ judges (distinct prompts/models) with conditionally independent errors given $z$.
\end{assumption}

\PROP{\textbf{Proposition (identification --- to prove).} Under Assumptions~\ref{as:anchor}--\ref{as:smooth},
and either \ref{as:raters} or a connected graph with anchors in every component, the confusion $\Pi$ and the
latent field $z$ are identified up to the ordering of roles fixed by the anchors. \emph{Idea:} anchors pin
$\pi_2$ (and, via connected bridges, $\pi_1$); graph smoothness turns each anchor's neighbourhood into repeated
views of correlated roles through the same instrument, playing the role of replication that a single judge
lacks. A precise statement under a block model of chain vs.\ distractor sub-graphs, with the estimable
information matrix, is the theoretical core.}

This is the sense in which \emph{structure identifies the measurement}: the corpus graph substitutes for the
replication or gold that is otherwise required to de-bias a systematic instrument.

\section{Inference and Calibration}\label{sec:infer}
\paragraph{Posterior.} The target is $\Pr(z,\Pi,\theta \mid \{g^{(\rho)}\}, A)$ under \eqref{eq:obs}--
\eqref{eq:prior}, with Dirichlet/ordered-probit priors on $\Pi$ (regularizing the bridge-bias) and a prior on
$\theta$. \PROP{Inference by blocked Gibbs / VI: Potts $z$-updates by graph message passing or a mean-field
GMRF relaxation; conjugate Dirichlet updates for $\Pi$; the anchors enter as soft evidence. Scales per query
($N{=}100$).}
\paragraph{Calibrated relevance.} The deliverable is the bias-corrected posterior $\Pr(r_i=1\mid g,A)$.
\PROP{\textbf{Calibration target.} This posterior should be calibrated (nominal coverage of relevance sets)
whereas the raw judge is not; and its plug-in into a downstream decision should dominate using $g$ directly.}
\paragraph{Value of a corrected oracle.} Under the companion paper's set-completion loss, replacing the raw
judge label with the corrected posterior in the GP update should reduce expected loss.
\PROP{\textbf{Go / no-go.} The model earns its keep only if correction improves a decision; this is the single
experiment that determines whether Paper B stands alone or folds back into the companion paper as a modeling
remark.}

\section{Empirical Grounding (what we already see)}\label{sec:evid}
\TESTED{The bias exists and is structured. A binary answer-relevance judge has gold-recall $\approx 0.35$
(bridge-blind); an explicitly hop-aware ordinal prompt raises gold-recall to $0.82$---i.e., role framing shifts
the confusion, exactly the $\alpha_1$ effect in \eqref{eq:probit}---yet precision stays low ($0.17$--$0.36$),
and recall degrades on longer chains and adversarial corpora ($0.89\to0.57$). These are the sufficient
statistics the model is built to explain.}
\PARTIAL{We have, per corpus, the joint distribution of judge scores, gold labels, and graph structure over
$\sim$tens of thousands of (query, passage) pairs---enough to \emph{fit} $\Pi$ against a gold-derived role
proxy and test role-dependence directly. This is the first, cheap experiment (Dig~1).}

\section{Related Work}
Measurement-error and misclassification models (Carroll et al.); latent-class rater models and crowdsourcing
aggregation (Dawid--Skene and Bayesian successors); Potts/GMRF priors on graphs; the emerging but largely
informal ``LLM-as-judge reliability'' literature. Our novelty: (i) bias that is \emph{systematic in a latent
role}, breaking conditional-random-error assumptions; (ii) \emph{structural identification} replacing
replication/gold; (iii) an evaluation tied to a downstream decision rather than to agreement rates.

\section{Discussion}
The theme---\emph{rigorous Bayesian treatment of imperfect LLM oracles}---extends beyond retrieval: the same
model shape covers LLM correctness verifiers (a companion line on text-to-SQL), automated evaluation, and weak
supervision, wherever a cheap biased oracle scores structured items. The corpus-structure-as-identification
device is the transportable idea: when you cannot replicate or collect gold, the dependence structure among
items can de-bias the instrument that scores them.
