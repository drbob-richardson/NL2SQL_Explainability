\begin{abstract}
A cheap ``oracle''---an LLM judge, an automated verifier, a weak annotator---measures a latent scientific state
$Z_v$ at each node $v$ of a known dependency structure, returning $Y_v$. We study the regime in which the
measurement channel \emph{aliases} two scientifically distinct states: $Y$ cannot separate them because their
node-level emission distributions coincide or nearly so. Our motivating pathology is \emph{bridge-blindness}: a
relevance judge grades a necessary \emph{bridge} passage exactly like an \emph{irrelevant} one, because it does
not directly answer the query. The question is not ``can we correct the judge?'' but the sharper statistical
one: \emph{when can the dependence structure distinguish latent states that the measurement channel itself
cannot?} We call this \textbf{structural de-aliasing}, and we separate two aliasing depths---\emph{path}
(single-measurement) and \emph{structural} (joint-neighbourhood)---showing the branching of the tree can de-alias
states no single path distinguishes ($L^\star_{\mathrm{struct}}<L^\star_{\mathrm{path}}$, a de Finetti /
overdispersion signature). A literature audit disciplines the claims: the qualitative backbone---exact aliasing
$=$ an emission-consistent \emph{lumpable} partition---is probabilistic bisimulation $=$ lumpability, and
``branching beats paths'' is the classical linear-vs-branching-time gap, so we \emph{demote} these to scaffolding.
The paper's weight rests on what is unclaimed: (i) a \emph{near-aliasing rate}---we prove the phase boundary
$\delta_E^2+d\,\delta_G^2$ with error exponent the shared-$s$ Chernoff information between the structural
signatures (verified numerically), where the one aliased-state precedent (Weiss--Nadler 2015) stops at
consistency; (ii) the \emph{aliasing framing} itself, which every distinct-emission tree-identifiability result
skips; (iii) an iso-information \emph{design} law pricing structure against replication and a second instrument;
(iv) \emph{functional} recovery $R=h(Z)$ without parameter identification; and (v) the information limit
$\mathcal I_G=I(R_v;Y_{N(v)}\mid Y_v,X_v)$ fixing when structure can help at all. The empirical failure that
prompted this redirection---structure barely helps retrieval once cosine is present---is not a defeat: it is the
regime $\mathcal I_G\!\to\!0$, an instance the theory predicts exactly.
\end{abstract}

\section{The problem, and why it is not the old one}
The previous conception of this paper asked whether corpus structure can \emph{correct} a bridge-blind LLM
relevance judge. Empirically it cannot do much in retrieval: a calibrated retriever prior recovers the judge's
missed bridges nearly as well as anything, and the graph adds little. That negative, however, exposes a deeper
and more general problem. The judge does not merely add noise; it makes two scientifically different states---a
\emph{necessary bridge} and an \emph{irrelevant} passage---produce the \emph{same} observation law. This is
\emph{differential misclassification} that is total on a pair of states: the measurement is \emph{aliased}.

\begin{intuition}
Ordinary measurement error blurs a signal; here the instrument \emph{collapses} two states onto one output
distribution. No amount of that instrument, or better calibration of it, separates them. The only remaining hope
is that the two states \emph{behave differently in the dependency structure}---a bridge sits next to direct
evidence, an irrelevant passage next to more irrelevant material---so that the \emph{neighbourhood} of
measurements distinguishes what the node measurement cannot. That is the question worth a theorem.
\end{intuition}

\paragraph{What is and is not new (summary; see \S\ref{sec:related}).} A literature audit sharpened the claims
considerably, and we state them conservatively. The \emph{qualitative} backbone is largely known: our structural
signature $Q_L$ is a \emph{branching-time} object, and ``$Q_L(a)=Q_L(b)\,\forall L\iff$ common emission-consistent
lumpable block'' is probabilistic bisimulation $=$ lumpability (Larsen--Skou 1991; Buchholz 1994;
Baier et al.\ 2005); ``branching distinguishes more than paths'' is the classical linear-vs-branching-time gap;
and the tree-identifiability engine (conditionally-i.i.d.\ siblings) is Chang (1996) and Allman--Matias--Rhodes
(2009). We therefore \emph{demote} the lumpability characterization to qualitative scaffolding and cite it as
such. The genuinely unclaimed territory, and the paper's intended centre, is \textbf{quantitative}: (i) a
\emph{near-aliasing rate}---a Chernoff/minimax law for how fast the branching neighbourhood separates
\emph{node-aliased} states as the transition-row gap $\delta_G$ and branching degree $d$ vary---which even the one
aliased-state paper (Weiss--Nadler 2015: two states, a line, consistency only, no rates, not the near-aliased
regime) leaves open; (ii) the \emph{aliasing} framing itself, since every tree-identifiability result assumes
\emph{distinct} emissions and so never de-aliases $O_a=O_b$; and (iii) the information limit $\mathcal I_G$ that
says when a cheap covariate leaves anything for structure to add. Functional recovery (\S\ref{sec:func}) is a
clean corollary in the lineage of estimable functions (Rao) and partial identification (Manski).

\subsection{Origin: structure as covariance (Paper A, the sibling CS/ML paper)}
The program began as a Bayesian active-retrieval method. Per query, a first-stage pool of $N$ candidates each
carries cheap features (cosine, BM25) and can be \emph{judged} (an expensive LLM call) to reveal relevance under a
budget $B$. The idea was ``structure-as-covariance'': the corpus graph that \emph{hurts} ranking (diffusion buries
the answer) \emph{helps} as a GP covariance---``connected passages have correlated relevance''---so a graph-kernel
GP (GMRF kernel $(I+\lambda\mathcal L)^{-1}$) propagates each judgment to connected candidates. Against passive
verify-top-$B$ and a cosine-kernel GP (BAGEL-lite):
\EVID{Under an \emph{oracle} judge, graph-GP-UCB beats both on multi-hop \emph{bridge} questions (2Wiki chained:
$+0.106\,[.095,.118]$ recall@$k$ over passive, $+0.077$ over cosine-GP at $B{=}2$), and is neutral on
\emph{independent} comparison questions---a sharp connectivity boundary. The gain concentrates where a gold is
\emph{buried} (cosine rank $\ge4$) \emph{and} connected (rank-5+ connected $+0.190$ vs $+0.020$ not-connected).
Normalizing the kernel to correlation (unit-diagonal) form \emph{doubled} the effect ($+0.122\,[.088,.155]$ at
$B{=}1$)---it equalizes hub variance so UCB explores the propagating anchors. UCB beats 1-/2-step VOI and
info-gain (kernel and acquisition are entangled: the graph needs UCB's focused exploration). After normalization,
the retrieval win converts to answers (F1 $+0.040\,[.018,.063]$).}
That paper (graph as \emph{covariance}, propagating \emph{belief}) is alive and coherent; it survives being handed
a perfect judge. \emph{This} paper is the other half.

\subsection{The turn: a real judge is bridge-blind (differential misclassification)}
Replacing the oracle with an off-the-shelf judge (gpt-4o-mini, ``does this passage answer the query?'') collapsed
the win. The reason is the phenomenon this paper is about:
\EVID{The judge is \emph{bridge-blind}: precision $0.64$ but recall $\mathbf{0.35}$, says-yes $0.12$ vs.\ gold
$0.23$. It grades a necessary \emph{bridge} passage (one that supplies a linking entity but does not itself answer
the query) exactly like an \emph{irrelevant} one. This is not random noise; it is error concentrated on a single
latent role---\emph{differential} misclassification, i.e.\ a node-level \emph{alias} $O_{\textsf{bridge}}\approx
O_{\textsf{irrelevant}}$. A graded ``hop-aware'' prompt that explicitly credits linking passages raised judge
recall $0.35\!\to\!0.75\!\to\!0.82$, confirming the bias is real and structured---but even with the fixed judge the
structure win did not return at $N{=}10$ (washout). It reappeared only in the \emph{deep-burial} regime $N{=}100$
(bridge at rank 20--90, beyond the embedding kernel's reach): graph$-$cosine $+0.039$ at $B{=}1$ on \emph{both}
datasets ($n{=}600$), chain-completion $+0.068$.}

\subsection{Paper B v1: the correction model, and its instructive real-data failure}
The first statistical formulation modelled the bias as a role-dependent confusion law with a Potts/GMRF prior
tying latent roles to the graph, and asked whether the posterior could \emph{correct} the judge.
\EVID{In a \emph{favorable simulation} (dense anchored clusters) it worked and structure was \emph{necessary}:
with no graph ($\theta{=}0$) bridge recall degrades $0.48\!\to\!0.14$ (worse than the raw judge); at $\theta{=}1$
it flips bridges up, AUC $0.965$; and \emph{even a misspecified emission still works}, so the graph, not the
measurement model, carries the correction. This gave a sharp thesis: \emph{when a biased oracle's errors mimic
true negatives, the measurements are structurally silent about the bias, so the correction must come from
inter-item dependence}. \textbf{But on real Hotpot/2Wiki data the graph correction is marginal.} The calibrated
retriever \emph{prior} does the recovery: AUC(all gold) $0.876$ (raw grade) $\to0.941$ (prior) $\to0.968$
(prior$+$graph)---the graph adds only $+0.027$; a symmetric-Potts graph term actually \emph{hurts}
($0.954\!\to\!0.81$). Diagnosis: real title graphs are \emph{sparse} ($\sim1.5$ degree), gold neighbourhoods are
\emph{balanced} (0.75 gold vs.\ 0.72 distractor), and only \textbf{35\%} of judge-missed bridges have a confident
grade-2 neighbour---a graph \emph{reachability ceiling}. The favorable sim assumed the dense clusters reality
lacks.}

\subsection{Why the failure produced this theory (not a retreat)}
Two lessons converted the dead end into the present direction. \emph{First}, the practical correction that works
(``trust the calibrated retriever over the raw grade'') needs neither the confusion matrix nor the graph---because
the cosine prior is a \emph{strong covariate for relevance}, so conditional on it the structural information is
near zero. That is precisely $\mathcal I_G\!\to\!0$ (Thm~\ref{thm:info}), a predicted corner, not an anomaly.
\emph{Second}, the question was never ``recover the confusion matrix'' (impossible---bridge-blindness makes the
emission self-obscuring, $O_{\textsf{bridge}}=O_{\textsf{irr}}$) but ``recover the \emph{decision}'' (relevance), a
\emph{functional} $R=h(Z)$. Reframing from \emph{correcting the instrument} to \emph{when can structure
distinguish states the instrument aliases} is structural de-aliasing, and it generalizes far beyond retrieval.
\EVID{The MuSiQue autopsy fixed the boundary condition: the mechanism is \emph{sound} (an oracle gold-clique graph
gives $+0.07$--$0.09$ even at 3 hops) but cheap graph construction \emph{fails} on adversarial corpora whose
distractors are made surface-similar to golds---no cosine/entity/decomposition graph recovers the chain
(gold-connectivity 0.35 vs.\ Hotpot 0.76). Structure-as-covariance helps \emph{iff the corpus's surface structure
aligns with the reasoning chain}---a measurable graph--chain alignment law.}

\section{Model}
Let $G=(V,\mathcal E)$ be a known graph (we develop the tree/chain case first). Each node carries a latent state
$Z_v\in[K]=\{1,\dots,K\}$; on a tree the field is a Markov random field with edge transition matrix $T$
($T_{cc'}=\Pr(Z_u\!=\!c'\mid Z_v\!=\!c)$) and root/initial law $\pi$. A measurement channel (the oracle) emits
\begin{equation}
  Y_v \mid Z_v=c \;\sim\; O_c \quad(\text{row } c \text{ of the }K\times M\text{ emission matrix } O).
\end{equation}
Optionally each node has cheap non-structural covariates $X_v$ (in retrieval, the cosine prior). The scientific
target is a \emph{functional} $R_v=h(Z_v)$ (in retrieval, $h(c)=\I\{c\ \text{relevant}\}$). Two states $a,b$ are
\emph{node-aliased} if $O_a=O_b$ (exactly) or $d(O_a,O_b)=\delta_E$ small (near). Bridge-blindness is
$O_{\textsf{bridge}}=O_{\textsf{irrelevant}}$ with $O_{\textsf{direct}}$ distinct.

\section{Structural signatures and two aliasing depths}
We must distinguish two objects that an earlier draft conflated. The first is the law of a \emph{single}
measurement at distance $L$; the second is the \emph{joint} law of the whole neighbourhood. The joint is strictly
richer, and the gap between them is where the interesting theory lives.
\begin{definition}[Path signature and structural signature]
The \emph{path signature} is $P_L(c)=\mathcal L(Y_w\mid Z_v=c)$ for a single node $w$ at path-distance $L$ from
$v$; on a tree $P_L(c)=(T^{L}O)_c$ (row $c$). The \emph{structural signature} is
$Q_L(c)=\mathcal L\big(Y_{B_L(v)}\mid Z_v=c\big)$, the \emph{joint} law of \emph{all} measurements in the
radius-$L$ ball. $P_0(c)=Q_0(c)=O_c$; $Q_L$ carries the dependence among the $Y_w$, $P_L$ only a marginal.
\end{definition}
\begin{definition}[Path vs.\ structural aliasing depth]
$L^\star_{\mathrm{path}}(a,b)=\min\{L:P_L(a)\neq P_L(b)\}$ and
$L^\star_{\mathrm{struct}}(a,b)=\min\{L:Q_L(a)\neq Q_L(b)\}$. Since a discriminating marginal implies a
discriminating joint but not conversely,
\[
   L^\star_{\mathrm{struct}}(a,b)\ \le\ L^\star_{\mathrm{path}}(a,b),
\]
and the inequality can be strict---the branching of the tree can de-alias states no single path distinguishes
(Thm~\ref{thm:branch}).
\end{definition}
If the judge is perfectly bridge-blind, $Q_0(\textsf{bridge})=Q_0(\textsf{irrelevant})$; de-aliasing asks whether
$Q_L$ (not merely $P_L$) separates them for some $L>0$.

\begin{theorem}[Path de-aliasing]\label{thm:dealias}
On a tree-indexed Markov chain, the path signature is row $c$ of $T^{L}O$:
$\Pr(Y_w=y\mid Z_v=c)=(T^{L}O)_{c,y}$. Hence if $O_a=O_b$ but $(T^{L}O)_a\neq(T^{L}O)_b$ for some $L$, then
$L^\star_{\mathrm{path}}(a,b)=\min\{L\ge0:(T^{L}O)_a\neq(T^{L}O)_b\}$ hops suffice for a \emph{single}
measurement to distinguish them.
\end{theorem}
\PROVEN{Markov property: $\Pr(Y_w\mid Z_v{=}c)=\sum_{c'}(T^{L})_{cc'}O_{c'}=(T^{L}O)_{c}$. In the bridge-blind
model with heterophilic $T$ (bridge$\to$direct), $O_{\textsf{irr}}=O_{\textsf{bridge}}$ but
$(TO)_{\textsf{irr}}=(0.64,0.20,0.16)\neq(TO)_{\textsf{bridge}}=(0.28,0.20,0.52)$, so
$L^\star_{\mathrm{path}}=1$. Verified: classifying the node-aliased pair from the node measurement alone is at
chance ($0.598$), from the neighbourhood $0.733$.}
\begin{intuition}
$L^\star_{\mathrm{path}}$ is ``how many hops until \emph{one} downstream measurement is marginally informative.''
Heterophily (aliased states have different neighbours) is what makes it finite and small.
\end{intuition}

\begin{theorem}[Branching de-aliasing: the joint beats every marginal]\label{thm:branch}
There exist tree-indexed models and node-aliased states $a,b$ with $L^\star_{\mathrm{path}}(a,b)=\infty$ (no
single measurement at any distance distinguishes them: $P_L(a)=P_L(b)$ for all $L$) yet
$L^\star_{\mathrm{struct}}(a,b)=2$. The mechanism is dependence: on a tree the \emph{immediate}-neighbour joint
factorizes, $Q_1(c)=(TO)_c^{\otimes\deg(v)}$, so radius-$1$ branching adds nothing beyond the marginal; but at
radius $\ge2$ siblings sharing a parent are conditionally dependent given $Z_v$, and their \emph{joint} can
differ across states whose every marginal coincides. The de-aliasing rate is then the Chernoff information of the
joint signatures, positive even though the per-measurement Chernoff information is exactly $0$.
\end{theorem}
\PROVEN{\emph{Explicit construction and verification} (\texttt{paperB\_branch\_sim.py}). Binary measurement;
centre state $c\in\{a,b\}$ acts through a hidden intermediate $U\sim T_c$ over three states with Bernoulli
measurement means $m=(.2,.5,.8)$, each emitting conditionally-i.i.d.\ children. Take $T_a=(.5,0,.5)$ and
$T_b=(0,1,0)$. Then every single-measurement marginal has mean $\sum_j T_c(j)m_j=0.5$ under \emph{both} states
(so $P_L(a)=P_L(b)$, $L^\star_{\mathrm{path}}=\infty$), while the pairwise correlation
$\E[Y_iY_k\mid c]=\sum_j T_c(j)m_j^2$ is $0.34$ vs.\ $0.25$---the sibling joint separates them. Numerically the
single-measurement Bayes error is exactly $0.5$ (chance), the two-sibling error $0.41$, and it decays as
$e^{-D\gamma}$ in the number $D$ of siblings with structural rate $\gamma\to0.089>0$, whereas the path Chernoff
information is $0$. Hence $L^\star_{\mathrm{struct}}=2<\infty=L^\star_{\mathrm{path}}$.}
\begin{intuition}
This is a de Finetti / overdispersion signature: state $a$ makes siblings \emph{agree} (a shared hidden cause
pushes them jointly low or jointly high), state $b$ makes them independent fair coins. Any one child looks like a
fair coin under both---you must see \emph{several children at once} to feel the correlation. Branching is not just
``more of the same measurement''; it exposes dependence, a strictly stronger channel.
\end{intuition}
\paragraph{Positioning.} The mechanism---identification from repeated conditionally-i.i.d.\ views of a hidden
node---is the engine of latent-tree identifiability (Chang 1996 on triples; Allman--Matias--Rhodes 2009 on
Kruskal/tensor; Choi et al.\ 2011), and abstractly ``branching $\sqsupset$ path'' is linear-vs-branching-time
(Baier et al.\ 2005). Our contribution is not that principle but its use for \emph{aliasing}: those results all
assume \emph{distinct} emissions and identify the full tree, whereas we drive the \emph{same} mechanism to
separate \emph{node-aliased} states $O_a=O_b$ and, in Thm~\ref{thm:rate}, attach a \emph{rate}. To our knowledge
the path-vs-structural aliasing dichotomy and the overdispersion de-aliasing signature are not stated in either
the phylogenetics/latent-tree or the concurrency literatures.

\begin{theorem}[Exact aliasing $=$ lumpable partition; \emph{scaffold, largely known}]\label{thm:lump}
Under a genericity condition on $O$, states $a,b$ are \emph{fully aliased} ($Q_L(a)=Q_L(b)$ for all $L$, hence
indistinguishable from any finite neighbourhood) iff they lie in the same block of the coarsest partition
$\mathcal P$ of $[K]$ that is (i) \emph{emission-consistent} ($O$ constant on blocks) and (ii) \emph{strongly
lumpable} for $T$ ($\sum_{c'\in B'}T_{cc'}$ constant over $c\in B$). No consistent estimator separates states in a
common block.
\end{theorem}
\SKETCH{\textbf{This is known and we cite it as such.} The characterization is exactly \emph{probabilistic
bisimulation} for labelled Markov chains (Larsen--Skou 1991), which equals \emph{strong lumpability}
(Kemeny--Snell 1960, Thm 6.3.2; Buchholz 1994) with the emission as label; the branching-vs-linear placement is
Baier et al.\ (2005). \emph{Forward:} emission-consistency $+$ lumpability make the block process Markov with
block-measurable emissions, so same-block states induce identical joint $Q_L$ for all $L$. \emph{Converse:} the
hard direction, controlling the \emph{joint} law (not merely marginals $P_L$, which by Thm~\ref{thm:branch} do not
suffice); the genericity condition rules out accidental cancellations in $O$. \textbf{We do not claim this as a
contribution}---it is the qualitative identifiability boundary against which the quantitative results are stated.
The one nuance worth flagging is that this \emph{tree} (branching) equivalence is strictly finer than the
\emph{string}/trace equivalence of classical HMM realization theory (Hankel-rank minimality: Carlyle--Paz 1971,
Vidyasagar 2011, Balle et al.\ 2015), precisely because siblings are dependent---the content of
Thm~\ref{thm:branch}.}

\section{Functional identifiability: decision recovery without parameter recovery}\label{sec:func}
\emph{This is the conceptual payoff.} The headline is not about bridges or judges; it is:
\begin{quote}\itshape
We need not identify the latent measurement model to identify the scientific decision target.
\end{quote}
The emission cannot see relevance ($O_{\textsf{bridge}}=O_{\textsf{irr}}$), so the confusion matrix and the role
labels are \emph{not} identified. Yet the functional $R=h(Z)$ can be, and that is all a decision needs.
\begin{theorem}[Functional identifiability]\label{cor:func}
The functional $R_v=h(Z_v)$ is identified from the radius-$L$ neighbourhood iff every pair $a,b$ with
$h(a)\neq h(b)$ is structurally de-aliased, $L^\star_{\mathrm{struct}}(a,b)\le L$. This can hold while the
underlying state is \emph{not} identified: aliased pairs $a,b$ with $h(a)=h(b)$ never need separating, and (by
Thm~\ref{thm:branch}) an $h$-splitting pair may be separated by the branching joint even when no single
measurement---hence no marginal likelihood term---distinguishes them. In the bridge-blind model the only
$h$-splitting node-aliased pair is $(\textsf{irrelevant},\textsf{bridge})$, de-aliased at radius $1$; relevance is
recoverable though the emission is blind to it and the role parameters are not.
\end{theorem}
\PROVEN{Identifying $h$ requires distinguishing only $h$-splitting pairs; apply Thms~\ref{thm:dealias}--
\ref{thm:branch} to exactly those pairs, and note $h$-collapsing aliased pairs are free.}
\begin{intuition}
Estimable functions of a nonidentified parameter are classical (Rao), and partial identification (Manski) usually
delivers a \emph{set} for a target under a nonidentified model; the crisp statement here---\emph{the recoverable
functionals are exactly those measurable w.r.t.\ the coarsest emission-consistent lumpable partition}---is the
natural corollary of Thm~\ref{thm:lump}, and the structural signature can \emph{point}-identify such a functional
even when the marginal likelihood cannot (Thm~\ref{thm:branch}). ``Recover the decision, not the parameter'' is
both the statistical selling point and the reason the retrieval application, which only ever needed the decision,
was never really asking for the (impossible) parameter recovery.
\end{intuition}

\section{The information limit: exactly when structure can help}
Let $X_v$ be cheap non-structural covariates. Define the \emph{incremental structural information}
\begin{equation}\label{eq:IG}
  \mathcal I_G \;=\; I\big(R_v;\,Y_{N(v)}\ \big|\ Y_v,\,X_v\big).
\end{equation}
\begin{theorem}[Information limit]\label{thm:info}
Under logarithmic loss, the reduction in optimal (Bayes) risk from adding the neighbourhood measurements is
exactly
\[
  \HH(R_v\mid Y_v,X_v)-\HH(R_v\mid Y_v,X_v,Y_{N(v)})=\mathcal I_G .
\]
In particular $\mathcal I_G=0\Rightarrow$ \emph{no} graph-based rule---however elaborate the MRF---improves optimal
log-loss prediction of $R_v$; and by Fano/Pinsker the improvement in $0$--$1$ Bayes risk is bounded by an
increasing function of $\mathcal I_G$.
\end{theorem}
\PROVEN{The identity is the definition of conditional mutual information. The $0$--$1$ bound follows from
Fano/Pinsker. \textbf{Verified regime-switch:} with $R=\I\{Z\ge1\}$ and a Gaussian covariate $X_v\sim\N(\mu_{Z_v},
\sigma)$ that separates relevant from irrelevant, the neighbour-gain (an $\mathcal I_G$ proxy in nats) is
$0.09$ (no $X$), $0.11$ (weak, $\sigma{=}1$), $0.10$ ($\sigma{=}0.5$), $0.007$ (strong, $\sigma{=}0.2$),
$0.000$ (near-perfect, $\sigma{=}0.1$): as the covariate becomes sufficient for $R$, $\mathcal I_G\to0$.}
\begin{intuition}
This single quantity explains all three things we saw. In the clean simulation there was no cheap covariate, so
$\mathcal I_G$ was large and structure rescued relevance. In real retrieval the cosine prior is a \emph{strong}
covariate---it already separates buried-bridge from distractor---so $\mathcal I_G$ is tiny and the graph adds
almost nothing. And when the covariate is sufficient, $\mathcal I_G=0$ and \emph{no} method can help. The
retrieval ``failure'' is the theorem's $\mathcal I_G\!\to\!0$ corner, not a bug.
\end{intuition}

\section{The near-aliasing rate (proved) and Bayesian weak identification}
The results above are qualitative (identified / not). T\&M wants \emph{rates and limits} in the near-aliased
regime, $\delta_E^2=H^2(O_a,O_b)\to0$ and $\delta_G^2=H^2\big((T^{L}O)_a,(T^{L}O)_b\big)\to0$
(squared Hellinger distances). The conditional-independence structure of the model makes this a clean product-of-channels
computation, and the conjectured phase boundary becomes a theorem.

\begin{theorem}[Near-aliasing rate and phase boundary]\label{thm:rate}
Test $H_a\!:Z_v=a$ against $H_b\!:Z_v=b$ (with $h(a)\neq h(b)$) from the node measurement $Y_v$ and $d$
\emph{conditionally-independent} neighbour measurements at radius $L$ (the immediate-neighbour / star geometry,
where the $Y_j$ are i.i.d.\ from $(T^{L}O)_c$ given $Z_v=c$; for $L=1$ this is exact---see the dependence caveat
below). Write the Chernoff \emph{divergence functions}
$C_E(s)=-\log\sum_y O_{a,y}^{s}O_{b,y}^{1-s}$ and $C_G(s)=-\log\sum_y (T^{L}O)_{a,y}^{s}(T^{L}O)_{b,y}^{1-s}$,
with Chernoff informations $C_E=\max_s C_E(s)$, $C_G=\max_s C_G(s)$ (optimizers $s_E^\star,s_G^\star$), and
Bhattacharyya coefficients $\rho_E=1-\tfrac12\delta_E^2$, $\rho_G=1-\tfrac12\delta_G^2$ (the $s{=}\tfrac12$ values).
Then for the equal-prior Bayes error $P_e$:
\begin{enumerate}[label=(\alph*),leftmargin=*,itemsep=0pt]
\item \textbf{(product factorization)} $P_c=O_c\otimes(T^{L}O)_c^{\otimes d}$, so the $s$-tilted affinity
multiplies: $\sum P_a^{s}P_b^{1-s}=\exp\!\big(-C_E(s)-d\,C_G(s)\big)$; at $s{=}\tfrac12$, $\rho=\rho_E\rho_G^{d}$;
\item \textbf{(bounds)} $\tfrac14\big(\rho_E\rho_G^{d}\big)^{2}\le P_e\le \tfrac12\,\rho_E\rho_G^{d}$, hence
$P_e=\exp\!\big(-\Theta(\delta_E^2+d\,\delta_G^2)\big)$;
\item \textbf{(sharp exponent, shared $s$)}
$P_e\asymp d^{-1/2}\exp\!\big(-\max_{s}\{C_E(s)+d\,C_G(s)\}\big)$ (Bahadur--Rao, under a nonlattice condition on
the per-neighbour log-likelihood ratio; a bounded oscillatory factor replaces the constant in the lattice case).
The exponent uses \emph{one shared} $s$: $\max_s\{C_E(s)+dC_G(s)\}\le C_E+dC_G$ in general, with equality only if
$s_E^\star=s_G^\star$; as $d\to\infty$ the optimizer $\to s_G^\star$ and the exponent is
$d\,C_G+C_E(s_G^\star)+o(1)$---the node contributes $C_E$ evaluated at the \emph{structural} optimizer, an $O(1)$
term, not $\max_s C_E$;
\item \textbf{(phase boundary)} for one node of degree $d$, $P_e\to0$ iff $\delta_E^2+d\,\delta_G^2\to\infty$; in
the near-aliased regime with $\delta_E$ bounded, iff $d\,\delta_G^2\to\infty$. For $n$ independent degree-$d$ stars
the effective information is $n(\delta_E^2+d\,\delta_G^2)$, separating \emph{replication depth} $n$ from
\emph{structural degree} $d$.
\end{enumerate}
\end{theorem}
\PROVEN{\emph{Proof.} (a) By the Markov property, given $Z_v=c$ the node emission and the $d$ neighbours are
mutually independent, each $\sim(T^{L}O)_c$; affinities of products multiply, and so does the $s$-tilted
integral. (b) Bhattacharyya upper bound $P_e\le\tfrac12\rho$ and Le~Cam lower bound
$P_e\ge\tfrac12(1-\mathrm{TV})\ge\tfrac14\rho^{2}$ with $\rho=\rho_E\rho_G^{d}$ and
$-\log\rho_\bullet=\tfrac12\delta_\bullet^2(1+o(1))$. (c) The log-likelihood ratio is a sum of $d$ i.i.d.\ neighbour
terms plus the node term; Chernoff's theorem gives exponent $\max_s\{C_E(s)+dC_G(s)\}$ (one $s$ tilts the whole
product), and the local CLT gives the $d^{-1/2}$ prefactor under the nonlattice condition. Convexity of $C_E(s)+
dC_G(s)$ and $C_G'(s_G^\star)=0$ give the $d\to\infty$ expansion. (d) Immediate from (b)--(c). \hfill$\square$\\[2pt]
\textbf{Verified} (\texttt{paperB\_rate\_sim.py}). \emph{Exact node alias} $\delta_E{=}0$ (all information
structural): $(T\!O)_a{=}(.64,.20,.16)$, $(T\!O)_b{=}(.28,.20,.52)$ give $C_G{=}0.0925$; a Bahadur--Rao fit
$-\log P_e=a\,d+p\log d+\text{const}$ ($d\le96$) recovers $a=0.0934$ (matches $C_G$) and $p=0.34$ (matches the
$\tfrac12$ prefactor). \emph{Shared-$s$ correction:} for an asymmetric node channel ($s_E^\star{=}0.68$) crossed
with a symmetric neighbour channel ($s_G^\star{=}0.50$), $\max_s\{C_E{+}C_G\}=0.845$ at the compromise
$s^\star{=}0.57$, strictly below the separate-max $0.878$---confirming the exponent is the shared-$s$ value, with
the gap $O((s_E^\star-s_G^\star)^2)$ vanishing near the alias.}
\begin{intuition}
The rate says exactly how ``buy-able'' de-aliasing is: each informative neighbour multiplies the odds of the
right call by a fixed factor $e^{C_G}$, so error falls geometrically in the number of neighbours---but the
per-neighbour exponent $C_G\approx\tfrac14\delta_G^2$ is tiny when the states are near-aliased, so you need
$\sim 1/\delta_G^2$ neighbours to separate them. That $1/\delta_G^2$ is the price of the alias.
\end{intuition}

\begin{corollary}[Iso-information: structure vs.\ replication vs.\ a second instrument]\label{cor:design}
Because independent channels contribute \emph{additively} to the error exponent (product factorization,
Thm~\ref{thm:rate}a), a design with $n$ replicated node measurements, $d$ informative neighbours, and $m$ readings
from a second instrument $O^{(2)}$ (partially breaking the alias, per-reading information $C_2$) has effective
local information
\[
   \mathcal C(n,d,m)\;=\;n\,C_E+d\,C_G+m\,C_2\quad(\text{leading order; shared-}s\text{ correction }o(1)\text{ near the alias}),
\]
and the \emph{iso-information surfaces} $\{n C_E+d C_G+m C_2=\text{const}\}$ give the exact substitution rates: one
neighbour is worth $C_G/C_E$ repeated node measurements and $C_G/C_2$ second-instrument readings. In the
near-aliased regime $C_E\to0$ (the node is useless), so structure and the second instrument are the \emph{only}
currencies, trading at $C_G/C_2=\delta_G^2/\delta_2^2$.
\end{corollary}
\PROVEN{Additivity of Chernoff exponents over independent channels (Thm~\ref{thm:rate}a) with the shared-$s$
caveat of Thm~\ref{thm:rate}c; the near-aliased limits are the Hellinger expansions.} This turns ``use a second
prompt/model'' from a corollary into a quantitative \emph{measurement-design} statement---how many extra readings
one unit of structure is worth---which is the methodological result a T\&M readership will want, and it positions
us squarely away from replication-only debiasing (anchor/paired designs): here structure is a first-class,
priced substitute for measurement.

\paragraph{Remaining rigor.} (a)--(d) are proved for the immediate-neighbour (degree-$d$ / star) case, which is
also the retrieval geometry. Two extensions complete the T\&M version: (i) \emph{general trees}---the affinity
still factorizes over the $d$ immediate subtrees and each subtree affinity is $\le$ its radius-$1$ value, so the
bound holds a fortiori; the exact deep-subtree Chernoff constant is the remaining computation; (ii) the
\emph{field} minimax (estimating $R$ over all nodes) follows from the pairwise rate by a standard Assouad/Fano
reduction. Neither is expected to change the boundary $n(\delta_E^2+d\,\delta_G^2)$ (replication depth $n$,
structural degree $d$).

\subsection{The local weak-identification regime (the Bayesian core to prove)}
Contraction/no-contraction is a coarse dichotomy; the interesting Bayesian object sits \emph{on} the phase
boundary. Index a sequence of models by $n$ (independent degree-$d$ stars) with structural separation shrinking at
the critical rate
\[
   \sqrt{n}\,\delta_{G,n}\to \kappa\in[0,\infty],
\]
so $\kappa=\infty$ is strong identification, $\kappa=0$ full aliasing, and $\kappa\in(0,\infty)$ a genuine
weak-identification continuum.
\begin{conjecture}[Nonstandard limit on the boundary]\label{conj:bayes}
Along $\sqrt n\,\delta_{G,n}\to\kappa$: (a) the local log-likelihood for the de-aliasing contrast converges (LAN
with a \emph{degree-weighted} information $d\,I_G^\star$) to a Gaussian shift experiment with signal $\propto
\kappa\sqrt d$, so the posterior for the functional $R$ has a \emph{nonstandard, prior-dependent} limit indexed by
$\kappa$---interpolating Bernstein--von Mises ($\kappa=\infty$) and prior ($\kappa=0$); (b) the emission-bias
\emph{nuisance} has a non-regular (prior-sensitive) limit while the \emph{functional} $R=h(Z)$ remains
decision-consistent---the Bayesian face of parameter-vs-decision recovery; (c) hence
$d_{\mathrm{TV}}\{\Pi_1(R\mid Y,G),\Pi_2(R\mid Y,G)\}\to$ an explicit function of $\kappa$ that is $0$ at
$\kappa=\infty$ and bounded away from $0$ for $\kappa<\infty$---a computable ``learned-vs-imposed correction''
diagnostic.
\end{conjecture}
\CONJ{Establish LAN for the tree-signature likelihood at the aliasing point (the score is the sibling-covariance
contrast of Thm~\ref{thm:branch}, so the information is $O(d)$---degree buys local information), then a
Bernstein--von Mises / limit-experiment analysis in the localized parameter. The nuisance non-regularity is a
cut-posterior statement. This is the Bayesian Analysis centre; it is genuinely nonstandard (weak identification),
not routine posterior contraction.}

\section{Roadmap: what must carry the paper}
Ordered by how much novelty each piece bears (audit-adjusted).
\begin{enumerate}[leftmargin=*,itemsep=1pt]
  \item \textbf{[CENTER] Near-aliasing rate (Thm~\ref{thm:rate}).} Proved + verified for the star; the phase
  boundary $\delta_E^2+d\delta_G^2$ and exponent $=$ shared-$s$ Chernoff are done. Finish: deep-subtree Chernoff on
  general trees and the Assouad/Fano \emph{field} minimax (Priority~4 in the review). This is the result with no
  prior owner---position vs.\ Weiss--Nadler (no rates) and Chang/AMR (distinct emissions).
  \item \textbf{[CENTER] Aliasing framing $+$ branching de-aliasing (Thm~\ref{thm:branch})} with the
  overdispersion signature; the object the tree-identifiability literature never treats because it assumes
  distinct emissions.
  \item \textbf{[PAYOFF] Functional recovery (Thm~\ref{cor:func})} and the \textbf{design corollary
  (Cor~\ref{cor:design})}: decision-not-parameter, and structure priced against replication/instruments.
  \item \textbf{[SCAFFOLD] Lumpability boundary (Thm~\ref{thm:lump})}---cite bisimulation$=$lumpability, do not
  claim; use only to state the identifiability boundary.
  \item \textbf{[BA ROUTE] Local weak identification (Conj~\ref{conj:bayes})}: LAN at the aliasing point, the
  nonstandard $\kappa$-indexed posterior, nuisance non-regularity. If this lands cleanly it may be the stronger
  route (Bayesian Analysis) even if the tree minimax gets technical.
  \item \textbf{[DISCIPLINE] $\mathcal I_G$ screen for a second application} (reasoning-DAG / SQL-AST /
  claim-evidence) \emph{before} modelling---build only where $\mathcal I_G$ is materially $>0$ after every cheap
  predictor.
\end{enumerate}

\paragraph{Ceiling assessment (audit-adjusted).} If the finished paper were mostly Thms~\ref{thm:dealias},
\ref{thm:lump} and a star calculation, a T\&M referee who knows concurrency theory would rightly say the
mathematics follows from bisimulation/lumpability and repeated-views identifiability. The legitimate T\&M shot is
\emph{the near-aliasing minimax rate on trees $+$ the aliasing framing $+$ functional recovery $+$ the design
corollary}; the Bayesian-Analysis shot is \emph{the local weak-identification asymptotics}. The retrieval failure
did not kill B---it revealed that the original theorem was aimed at the easy (already-solved) part of the
parameter space and handed us the near-aliased boundary, which is the part nobody owns.
