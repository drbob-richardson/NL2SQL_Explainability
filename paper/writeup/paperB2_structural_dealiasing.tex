\begin{abstract}
We study inference when a measurement channel \emph{nearly aliases} scientifically distinct latent states---their
node-level emission distributions coincide or nearly so---but the states participate differently in a known
dependency structure. Our main results are quantitative. We characterize the \emph{rate} at which structural
observations de-alias the states: for a target node with $d$ conditionally-independent structural views the
effective information combines measurement and structural separation as $\delta_E^2+d\,\delta_G^2$, with error
decaying at the shared-$s$ Chernoff exponent between the structural signatures; and we lift this to a \emph{field
minimax theorem}---estimating the functional field $\{h(Z_v)\}$ over a tree at minimax rate, with matching
Assouad lower and belief-propagation upper bounds and a clean transition between measurement-dominated,
structure-dominated, and unidentifiable regimes. We derive a \emph{measurement-design} law that prices structural
observations against replication and additional instruments (first-order $nC_E+dC_G+mC_2$). A local
weak-identification analysis reveals a \emph{singular} regime---by an exchangeability symmetry the likelihood is
\emph{even} in the separation, so the identifiable parameter is $\psi=\delta_G^2$ (estimable at the singular rate
$n^{-1/4}$ in $\delta_G$) on the boundary $\sqrt n\,\delta_{G,n}^2\to\lambda$, with a nonstandard truncated-Gaussian
posterior. Crucially, recovery is \emph{two-stage}: structure identifies the de-aliasing \emph{magnitude} and the
\emph{unordered} state pair, while a single anchor (or the prior) supplies the \emph{orientation} that fixes the
decision target $R=h(Z)$---so the functional is decision-consistent \emph{given} an anchor, and orientation costs
$O(1)$ external bits regardless of $n$. Local structural richness ($d\,\delta_G^2$, classification) and population
information ($\lambda$, learning the map) are distinct axes, yielding a two-dimensional phase diagram with two
separate failure modes. Exact (as opposed to near-) aliasing is governed by the known equivalence of emission-consistent
\emph{lumpability} and probabilistic bisimulation, which we use as scaffolding rather than a contribution; the
branching-beats-paths phenomenon that makes the structural signature richer than any single path is likewise the
classical linear-vs-branching-time gap. Exact (as opposed to near-) aliasing is governed by the known equivalence of emission-consistent
\emph{lumpability} and probabilistic bisimulation, which we use as scaffolding rather than a contribution; the
branching-beats-paths phenomenon that makes the structural signature richer than any single path is likewise the
classical linear-vs-branching-time gap. The motivating pathology is an LLM relevance judge that grades a necessary
\emph{bridge} passage exactly like an \emph{irrelevant} one (\emph{bridge-blindness}); the empirical fact that
structure barely helps retrieval once a cosine prior is present is not a defeat but the predicted corner
$\mathcal I_G=I(R_v;Y_{N(v)}\mid Y_v,X_v)\to0$ of the same theory.
\end{abstract}

\section{The problem, and why it is not the old one}
The previous conception of this paper asked whether corpus structure can \emph{correct} a bridge-blind LLM
relevance judge. Empirically it cannot do much in retrieval: a calibrated retriever prior recovers the judge's
missed bridges nearly as well as anything, and the graph adds little. That negative, however, exposes a deeper
and more general problem. The judge does not merely add noise; it makes two scientifically different states---a
\emph{necessary bridge} and an \emph{irrelevant} passage---produce the \emph{same} observation law. This is
\emph{differential misclassification} that is total on a pair of states: the measurement is \emph{aliased}.

\begin{intuition}
Ordinary measurement error blurs a signal; here the instrument \emph{collapses} two states onto one output
distribution. No amount of that instrument, or better calibration of it, separates them. The only remaining hope
is that the two states \emph{behave differently in the dependency structure}---a bridge sits next to direct
evidence, an irrelevant passage next to more irrelevant material---so that the \emph{neighbourhood} of
measurements distinguishes what the node measurement cannot. That is the question worth a theorem.
\end{intuition}

\paragraph{What is and is not new (summary; see \S\ref{sec:related}).} A literature audit sharpened the claims
considerably, and we state them conservatively. The \emph{qualitative} backbone is largely known: our structural
signature $Q_L$ is a \emph{branching-time} object, and ``$Q_L(a)=Q_L(b)\,\forall L\iff$ common emission-consistent
lumpable block'' is probabilistic bisimulation $=$ lumpability (Larsen--Skou 1991; Buchholz 1994;
Baier et al.\ 2005); ``branching distinguishes more than paths'' is the classical linear-vs-branching-time gap;
and the tree-identifiability engine (conditionally-i.i.d.\ siblings) is Chang (1996) and Allman--Matias--Rhodes
(2009). We therefore \emph{demote} the lumpability characterization to qualitative scaffolding and cite it as
such. The genuinely unclaimed territory, and the paper's intended centre, is \textbf{quantitative}: (i) a
\emph{near-aliasing rate}---a Chernoff/minimax law for how fast the branching neighbourhood separates
\emph{node-aliased} states as the transition-row gap $\delta_G$ and branching degree $d$ vary---which even the one
aliased-state paper (Weiss--Nadler 2015: two states, a line, consistency only, no rates, not the near-aliased
regime) leaves open; (ii) the \emph{aliasing} framing itself, since every tree-identifiability result assumes
\emph{distinct} emissions and so never de-aliases $O_a=O_b$; and (iii) the information limit $\mathcal I_G$ that
says when a cheap covariate leaves anything for structure to add. Functional recovery (\S\ref{sec:func}) is a
clean corollary in the lineage of estimable functions (Rao) and partial identification (Manski).

\subsection{Origin: structure as covariance (Paper A, the sibling CS/ML paper)}
The program began as a Bayesian active-retrieval method. Per query, a first-stage pool of $N$ candidates each
carries cheap features (cosine, BM25) and can be \emph{judged} (an expensive LLM call) to reveal relevance under a
budget $B$. The idea was ``structure-as-covariance'': the corpus graph that \emph{hurts} ranking (diffusion buries
the answer) \emph{helps} as a GP covariance---``connected passages have correlated relevance''---so a graph-kernel
GP (GMRF kernel $(I+\lambda\mathcal L)^{-1}$) propagates each judgment to connected candidates. Against passive
verify-top-$B$ and a cosine-kernel GP (BAGEL-lite):
\EVID{Under an \emph{oracle} judge, graph-GP-UCB beats both on multi-hop \emph{bridge} questions (2Wiki chained:
$+0.106\,[.095,.118]$ recall@$k$ over passive, $+0.077$ over cosine-GP at $B{=}2$), and is neutral on
\emph{independent} comparison questions---a sharp connectivity boundary. The gain concentrates where a gold is
\emph{buried} (cosine rank $\ge4$) \emph{and} connected (rank-5+ connected $+0.190$ vs $+0.020$ not-connected).
Normalizing the kernel to correlation (unit-diagonal) form \emph{doubled} the effect ($+0.122\,[.088,.155]$ at
$B{=}1$)---it equalizes hub variance so UCB explores the propagating anchors. UCB beats 1-/2-step VOI and
info-gain (kernel and acquisition are entangled: the graph needs UCB's focused exploration). After normalization,
the retrieval win converts to answers (F1 $+0.040\,[.018,.063]$).}
That paper (graph as \emph{covariance}, propagating \emph{belief}) is alive and coherent; it survives being handed
a perfect judge. \emph{This} paper is the other half.

\subsection{The turn: a real judge is bridge-blind (differential misclassification)}
Replacing the oracle with an off-the-shelf judge (gpt-4o-mini, ``does this passage answer the query?'') collapsed
the win. The reason is the phenomenon this paper is about:
\EVID{The judge is \emph{bridge-blind}: precision $0.64$ but recall $\mathbf{0.35}$, says-yes $0.12$ vs.\ gold
$0.23$. It grades a necessary \emph{bridge} passage (one that supplies a linking entity but does not itself answer
the query) exactly like an \emph{irrelevant} one. This is not random noise; it is error concentrated on a single
latent role---\emph{differential} misclassification, i.e.\ a node-level \emph{alias} $O_{\textsf{bridge}}\approx
O_{\textsf{irrelevant}}$. A graded ``hop-aware'' prompt that explicitly credits linking passages raised judge
recall $0.35\!\to\!0.75\!\to\!0.82$, confirming the bias is real and structured---but even with the fixed judge the
structure win did not return at $N{=}10$ (washout). It reappeared only in the \emph{deep-burial} regime $N{=}100$
(bridge at rank 20--90, beyond the embedding kernel's reach): graph$-$cosine $+0.039$ at $B{=}1$ on \emph{both}
datasets ($n{=}600$), chain-completion $+0.068$.}

\subsection{Paper B v1: the correction model, and its instructive real-data failure}
The first statistical formulation modelled the bias as a role-dependent confusion law with a Potts/GMRF prior
tying latent roles to the graph, and asked whether the posterior could \emph{correct} the judge.
\EVID{In a \emph{favorable simulation} (dense anchored clusters) it worked and structure was \emph{necessary}:
with no graph ($\theta{=}0$) bridge recall degrades $0.48\!\to\!0.14$ (worse than the raw judge); at $\theta{=}1$
it flips bridges up, AUC $0.965$; and \emph{even a misspecified emission still works}, so the graph, not the
measurement model, carries the correction. This gave a sharp thesis: \emph{when a biased oracle's errors mimic
true negatives, the measurements are structurally silent about the bias, so the correction must come from
inter-item dependence}. \textbf{But on real Hotpot/2Wiki data the graph correction is marginal.} The calibrated
retriever \emph{prior} does the recovery: AUC(all gold) $0.876$ (raw grade) $\to0.941$ (prior) $\to0.968$
(prior$+$graph)---the graph adds only $+0.027$; a symmetric-Potts graph term actually \emph{hurts}
($0.954\!\to\!0.81$). Diagnosis: real title graphs are \emph{sparse} ($\sim1.5$ degree), gold neighbourhoods are
\emph{balanced} (0.75 gold vs.\ 0.72 distractor), and only \textbf{35\%} of judge-missed bridges have a confident
grade-2 neighbour---a graph \emph{reachability ceiling}. The favorable sim assumed the dense clusters reality
lacks.}

\subsection{Why the failure produced this theory (not a retreat)}
Two lessons converted the dead end into the present direction. \emph{First}, the practical correction that works
(``trust the calibrated retriever over the raw grade'') needs neither the confusion matrix nor the graph---because
the cosine prior is a \emph{strong covariate for relevance}, so conditional on it the structural information is
near zero. That is precisely $\mathcal I_G\!\to\!0$ (Thm~\ref{thm:info}), a predicted corner, not an anomaly.
\emph{Second}, the question was never ``recover the confusion matrix'' (impossible---bridge-blindness makes the
emission self-obscuring, $O_{\textsf{bridge}}=O_{\textsf{irr}}$) but ``recover the \emph{decision}'' (relevance), a
\emph{functional} $R=h(Z)$. Reframing from \emph{correcting the instrument} to \emph{when can structure
distinguish states the instrument aliases} is structural de-aliasing, and it generalizes far beyond retrieval.
\EVID{The MuSiQue autopsy fixed the boundary condition: the mechanism is \emph{sound} (an oracle gold-clique graph
gives $+0.07$--$0.09$ even at 3 hops) but cheap graph construction \emph{fails} on adversarial corpora whose
distractors are made surface-similar to golds---no cosine/entity/decomposition graph recovers the chain
(gold-connectivity 0.35 vs.\ Hotpot 0.76). Structure-as-covariance helps \emph{iff the corpus's surface structure
aligns with the reasoning chain}---a measurable graph--chain alignment law.}

\section{Model}
Let $G=(V,\mathcal E)$ be a known graph (we develop the tree/chain case first). Each node carries a latent state
$Z_v\in[K]=\{1,\dots,K\}$; on a tree the field is a Markov random field with edge transition matrix $T$
($T_{cc'}=\Pr(Z_u\!=\!c'\mid Z_v\!=\!c)$) and root/initial law $\pi$. A measurement channel (the oracle) emits
\begin{equation}
  Y_v \mid Z_v=c \;\sim\; O_c \quad(\text{row } c \text{ of the }K\times M\text{ emission matrix } O).
\end{equation}
Optionally each node has cheap non-structural covariates $X_v$ (in retrieval, the cosine prior). The scientific
target is a \emph{functional} $R_v=h(Z_v)$ (in retrieval, $h(c)=\I\{c\ \text{relevant}\}$). Two states $a,b$ are
\emph{node-aliased} if $O_a=O_b$ (exactly) or $d(O_a,O_b)=\delta_E$ small (near). Bridge-blindness is
$O_{\textsf{bridge}}=O_{\textsf{irrelevant}}$ with $O_{\textsf{direct}}$ distinct.

\section{Structural signatures and two aliasing depths}
We must distinguish two objects that an earlier draft conflated. The first is the law of a \emph{single}
measurement at distance $L$; the second is the \emph{joint} law of the whole neighbourhood. The joint is strictly
richer, and the gap between them is where the interesting theory lives.
\begin{definition}[Path signature and structural signature]
The \emph{path signature} is $P_L(c)=\mathcal L(Y_w\mid Z_v=c)$ for a single node $w$ at path-distance $L$ from
$v$; on a tree $P_L(c)=(T^{L}O)_c$ (row $c$). The \emph{structural signature} is
$Q_L(c)=\mathcal L\big(Y_{B_L(v)}\mid Z_v=c\big)$, the \emph{joint} law of \emph{all} measurements in the
radius-$L$ ball. $P_0(c)=Q_0(c)=O_c$; $Q_L$ carries the dependence among the $Y_w$, $P_L$ only a marginal.
\end{definition}
\begin{definition}[Path vs.\ structural aliasing depth]
$L^\star_{\mathrm{path}}(a,b)=\min\{L:P_L(a)\neq P_L(b)\}$ and
$L^\star_{\mathrm{struct}}(a,b)=\min\{L:Q_L(a)\neq Q_L(b)\}$. Since a discriminating marginal implies a
discriminating joint but not conversely,
\[
   L^\star_{\mathrm{struct}}(a,b)\ \le\ L^\star_{\mathrm{path}}(a,b),
\]
and the inequality can be strict---the branching of the tree can de-alias states no single path distinguishes
(Thm~\ref{thm:branch}).
\end{definition}
If the judge is perfectly bridge-blind, $Q_0(\textsf{bridge})=Q_0(\textsf{irrelevant})$; de-aliasing asks whether
$Q_L$ (not merely $P_L$) separates them for some $L>0$.

\begin{theorem}[Path de-aliasing]\label{thm:dealias}
On a tree-indexed Markov chain, the path signature is row $c$ of $T^{L}O$:
$\Pr(Y_w=y\mid Z_v=c)=(T^{L}O)_{c,y}$. Hence if $O_a=O_b$ but $(T^{L}O)_a\neq(T^{L}O)_b$ for some $L$, then
$L^\star_{\mathrm{path}}(a,b)=\min\{L\ge0:(T^{L}O)_a\neq(T^{L}O)_b\}$ hops suffice for a \emph{single}
measurement to distinguish them.
\end{theorem}
\PROVEN{Markov property: $\Pr(Y_w\mid Z_v{=}c)=\sum_{c'}(T^{L})_{cc'}O_{c'}=(T^{L}O)_{c}$. In the bridge-blind
model with heterophilic $T$ (bridge$\to$direct), $O_{\textsf{irr}}=O_{\textsf{bridge}}$ but
$(TO)_{\textsf{irr}}=(0.64,0.20,0.16)\neq(TO)_{\textsf{bridge}}=(0.28,0.20,0.52)$, so
$L^\star_{\mathrm{path}}=1$. Verified: classifying the node-aliased pair from the node measurement alone is at
chance ($0.598$), from the neighbourhood $0.733$.}
\begin{intuition}
$L^\star_{\mathrm{path}}$ is ``how many hops until \emph{one} downstream measurement is marginally informative.''
Heterophily (aliased states have different neighbours) is what makes it finite and small.
\end{intuition}

\begin{theorem}[Branching de-aliasing: the joint beats every marginal]\label{thm:branch}
There is a two-layer latent branching model and node-aliased states $a,b$ in which \emph{no single observed
measurement distinguishes them} ($P(a)=P(b)$ marginally at the observed layer) yet the joint law of two siblings
does, so $L^\star_{\mathrm{struct}}=2$. The mechanism is dependence: given the centre, \emph{immediate} children
are conditionally independent, so their joint factorizes and adds nothing beyond the marginal; but children
sharing a hidden intermediate are conditionally dependent, and their joint can differ across states whose every
marginal coincides. The de-aliasing rate is the Chernoff information of the joint signatures, positive even though
the per-measurement Chernoff information is exactly $0$.
\end{theorem}
\PROVEN{\emph{Explicit construction and verification} (\texttt{paperB\_branch\_sim.py}). Binary measurement;
centre class $c\in\{a,b\}$ acts through a hidden intermediate $U\sim T_c$ over three states with Bernoulli means
$m=(.2,.5,.8)$, each emitting conditionally-i.i.d.\ children. Take $T_a=(.5,0,.5)$, $T_b=(0,1,0)$. Every
single child has mean $\sum_j T_c(j)m_j=0.5$ under \emph{both} classes (single-measurement Bayes error exactly
$0.5$), while the pairwise correlation $\E[Y_iY_k\mid c]=\sum_j T_c(j)m_j^2$ is $0.34$ vs.\ $0.25$; the two-sibling
error is $0.41$ and decays as $e^{-D\gamma}$ in the sibling count $D$ with rate $\gamma\to0.089>0$, while the path
Chernoff information is $0$.}
\SKETCH{\textbf{Honest scope of ``$L^\star_{\mathrm{path}}=\infty$'' (a point a referee will press).} The
construction above is a \emph{two-layer} model, and the claim it rigorously supports is that \emph{every
single-measurement marginal coincides}, so no one observation ever separates $a,b$. Realizing this inside a single
\emph{homogeneous} recurrent chain---literally $(T^{L}O)_a=(T^{L}O)_b$ for all $L$ while the tree law differs---
requires a pair that is \emph{trace-equivalent but not bisimilar}: a zero-sum direction in the \emph{unobservable}
subspace that is \emph{not} a lumpable block. (With the naive route the direction is a left eigenvector of $T$,
which forces emission-consistent lumpability, hence \emph{full} aliasing---so a genuinely strict
$L^\star_{\mathrm{struct}}<L^\star_{\mathrm{path}}=\infty$ needs the unobservable subspace to strictly exceed the
lumpable one.) That strict gap is exactly the linear-vs-branching separation (Baier et al.\ 2005), and exhibiting
the minimal homogeneous witness is a clean to-do; the two-layer construction is the economical proof of the
phenomenon.}
\begin{intuition}
This is a de Finetti / overdispersion signature: state $a$ makes siblings \emph{agree} (a shared hidden cause
pushes them jointly low or jointly high), state $b$ makes them independent fair coins. Any one child looks like a
fair coin under both---you must see \emph{several children at once} to feel the correlation. Branching is not just
``more of the same measurement''; it exposes dependence, a strictly stronger channel.
\end{intuition}
\paragraph{Positioning.} The mechanism---identification from repeated conditionally-i.i.d.\ views of a hidden
node---is the engine of latent-tree identifiability (Chang 1996 on triples; Allman--Matias--Rhodes 2009 on
Kruskal/tensor; Choi et al.\ 2011), and abstractly ``branching $\sqsupset$ path'' is linear-vs-branching-time
(Baier et al.\ 2005). Our contribution is not that principle but its use for \emph{aliasing}: those results all
assume \emph{distinct} emissions and identify the full tree, whereas we drive the \emph{same} mechanism to
separate \emph{node-aliased} states $O_a=O_b$ and, in Thm~\ref{thm:rate}, attach a \emph{rate}. To our knowledge
the path-vs-structural aliasing dichotomy and the overdispersion de-aliasing signature are not stated in either
the phylogenetics/latent-tree or the concurrency literatures.

\begin{theorem}[Exact aliasing $=$ lumpable partition; \emph{scaffold, largely known}]\label{thm:lump}
Under a genericity condition on $O$, states $a,b$ are \emph{fully aliased} ($Q_L(a)=Q_L(b)$ for all $L$, hence
indistinguishable from any finite neighbourhood) iff they lie in the same block of the coarsest partition
$\mathcal P$ of $[K]$ that is (i) \emph{emission-consistent} ($O$ constant on blocks) and (ii) \emph{strongly
lumpable} for $T$ ($\sum_{c'\in B'}T_{cc'}$ constant over $c\in B$). No consistent estimator separates states in a
common block.
\end{theorem}
\SKETCH{\textbf{This is known and we cite it as such.} The characterization is exactly \emph{probabilistic
bisimulation} for labelled Markov chains (Larsen--Skou 1991), which equals \emph{strong lumpability}
(Kemeny--Snell 1960, Thm 6.3.2; Buchholz 1994) with the emission as label; the branching-vs-linear placement is
Baier et al.\ (2005). \emph{Forward:} emission-consistency $+$ lumpability make the block process Markov with
block-measurable emissions, so same-block states induce identical joint $Q_L$ for all $L$. \emph{Converse:} the
hard direction, controlling the \emph{joint} law (not merely marginals $P_L$, which by Thm~\ref{thm:branch} do not
suffice); the genericity condition rules out accidental cancellations in $O$. \textbf{We do not claim this as a
contribution}---it is the qualitative identifiability boundary against which the quantitative results are stated.
The one nuance worth flagging is that this \emph{tree} (branching) equivalence is strictly finer than the
\emph{string}/trace equivalence of classical HMM realization theory (Hankel-rank minimality: Carlyle--Paz 1971,
Vidyasagar 2011, Balle et al.\ 2015), precisely because siblings are dependent---the content of
Thm~\ref{thm:branch}.}

\section{Functional identifiability: decision recovery without parameter recovery}\label{sec:func}
\emph{This is the conceptual payoff.} The headline is not about bridges or judges; it is:
\begin{quote}\itshape
We need not identify the latent measurement model to identify the scientific decision target.
\end{quote}
The emission cannot see relevance ($O_{\textsf{bridge}}=O_{\textsf{irr}}$), so the confusion matrix and the role
labels are \emph{not} identified. Yet the functional $R=h(Z)$ can be, and that is all a decision needs.
\begin{theorem}[Functional identifiability]\label{cor:func}
The functional $R_v=h(Z_v)$ is identified from the radius-$L$ neighbourhood iff every pair $a,b$ with
$h(a)\neq h(b)$ is structurally de-aliased, $L^\star_{\mathrm{struct}}(a,b)\le L$. This can hold while the
underlying state is \emph{not} identified: aliased pairs $a,b$ with $h(a)=h(b)$ never need separating, and (by
Thm~\ref{thm:branch}) an $h$-splitting pair may be separated by the branching joint even when no single
measurement---hence no marginal likelihood term---distinguishes them. In the bridge-blind model the only
$h$-splitting node-aliased pair is $(\textsf{irrelevant},\textsf{bridge})$, de-aliased at radius $1$; relevance is
recoverable though the emission is blind to it and the role parameters are not.
\end{theorem}
\PROVEN{Identifying $h$ requires distinguishing only $h$-splitting pairs; apply Thms~\ref{thm:dealias}--
\ref{thm:branch} to exactly those pairs, and note $h$-collapsing aliased pairs are free.}
\begin{intuition}
Estimable functions of a nonidentified parameter are classical (Rao), and partial identification (Manski) usually
delivers a \emph{set} for a target under a nonidentified model; the crisp statement here---\emph{the recoverable
functionals are exactly those measurable w.r.t.\ the coarsest emission-consistent lumpable partition}---is the
natural corollary of Thm~\ref{thm:lump}, and the structural signature can \emph{point}-identify such a functional
even when the marginal likelihood cannot (Thm~\ref{thm:branch}). ``Recover the decision, not the parameter'' is
both the statistical selling point and the reason the retrieval application, which only ever needed the decision,
was never really asking for the (impossible) parameter recovery.
\end{intuition}

\section{The information limit: exactly when structure can help}
Let $X_v$ be cheap non-structural covariates. Define the \emph{incremental structural information}
\begin{equation}\label{eq:IG}
  \mathcal I_G \;=\; I\big(R_v;\,Y_{N(v)}\ \big|\ Y_v,\,X_v\big).
\end{equation}
\begin{theorem}[Information limit]\label{thm:info}
Under logarithmic loss, the reduction in optimal (Bayes) risk from adding the neighbourhood measurements is
exactly
\[
  \HH(R_v\mid Y_v,X_v)-\HH(R_v\mid Y_v,X_v,Y_{N(v)})=\mathcal I_G .
\]
In particular $\mathcal I_G=0\Rightarrow$ \emph{no} graph-based rule---however elaborate the MRF---improves optimal
log-loss prediction of $R_v$. \emph{Quantitatively}, by the conditional Pinsker inequality the expected
posterior movement is bounded,
\[
   \E\big\|\Pr(R_v\!\mid\!Y_v,X_v,Y_{N(v)})-\Pr(R_v\!\mid\!Y_v,X_v)\big\|_{\mathrm{TV}}\ \le\ \sqrt{\mathcal I_G/2},
\]
so for \emph{any} bounded decision loss the improvement in optimal Bayes risk from the neighbourhood is at most of
order $\sqrt{\mathcal I_G/2}$; in particular $0\le\mathcal R^\ast(Y_v,X_v)-\mathcal R^\ast(Y_v,X_v,Y_{N(v)})\le
\sqrt{\mathcal I_G/2}$ for $0$--$1$ loss.
\end{theorem}
\PROVEN{The identity is conditional mutual information; the TV bound is conditional Pinsker
($\|\cdot\|_{\mathrm{TV}}\le\sqrt{\mathrm{KL}/2}$) with $\E\,\mathrm{KL}=\mathcal I_G$, and any bounded loss is
$\mathrm{TV}$-Lipschitz in the posterior. \textbf{Verified regime-switch:} with $R=\I\{Z\ge1\}$ and a Gaussian
covariate $X_v\sim\N(\mu_{Z_v},\sigma)$, the neighbour-gain (an $\mathcal I_G$ proxy in nats) is $0.09$ (no $X$),
$0.11$ ($\sigma{=}1$), $0.10$ ($\sigma{=}0.5$), $0.007$ ($\sigma{=}0.2$), $0.000$ ($\sigma{=}0.1$): as the
covariate becomes sufficient for $R$, $\mathcal I_G\to0$.}
\begin{intuition}
This single quantity explains all three things we saw. In the clean simulation there was no cheap covariate, so
$\mathcal I_G$ was large and structure rescued relevance. In real retrieval the cosine prior is a \emph{strong}
covariate---it already separates buried-bridge from distractor---so $\mathcal I_G$ is tiny and the graph adds
almost nothing. And when the covariate is sufficient, $\mathcal I_G=0$ and \emph{no} method can help. The
retrieval ``failure'' is the theorem's $\mathcal I_G\!\to\!0$ corner, not a bug.
\end{intuition}

\section{The near-aliasing rate (proved) and Bayesian weak identification}
The results above are qualitative (identified / not). T\&M wants \emph{rates and limits} in the near-aliased
regime, $\delta_E^2=H^2(O_a,O_b)\to0$ and $\delta_G^2=H^2\big((T^{L}O)_a,(T^{L}O)_b\big)\to0$
(squared Hellinger distances). The conditional-independence structure of the model makes this a clean product-of-channels
computation, and the conjectured phase boundary becomes a theorem.

\begin{theorem}[Near-aliasing rate and phase boundary]\label{thm:rate}
Test $H_a\!:Z_v=a$ against $H_b\!:Z_v=b$ (with $h(a)\neq h(b)$) from the node measurement $Y_v$ and $d$
\emph{conditionally-independent} neighbour measurements at radius $L$ (the immediate-neighbour / star geometry,
where the $Y_j$ are i.i.d.\ from $(T^{L}O)_c$ given $Z_v=c$; for $L=1$ this is exact---see the dependence caveat
below). Write the Chernoff \emph{divergence functions}
$C_E(s)=-\log\sum_y O_{a,y}^{s}O_{b,y}^{1-s}$ and $C_G(s)=-\log\sum_y (T^{L}O)_{a,y}^{s}(T^{L}O)_{b,y}^{1-s}$,
with Chernoff informations $C_E=\max_s C_E(s)$, $C_G=\max_s C_G(s)$ (optimizers $s_E^\star,s_G^\star$), and
Bhattacharyya coefficients $\rho_E=1-\tfrac12\delta_E^2$, $\rho_G=1-\tfrac12\delta_G^2$ (the $s{=}\tfrac12$ values).
Then for the equal-prior Bayes error $P_e$:
\begin{enumerate}[label=(\alph*),leftmargin=*,itemsep=0pt]
\item \textbf{(product factorization)} $P_c=O_c\otimes(T^{L}O)_c^{\otimes d}$, so the $s$-tilted affinity
multiplies: $\sum P_a^{s}P_b^{1-s}=\exp\!\big(-C_E(s)-d\,C_G(s)\big)$; at $s{=}\tfrac12$, $\rho=\rho_E\rho_G^{d}$;
\item \textbf{(bounds)} $\tfrac14\big(\rho_E\rho_G^{d}\big)^{2}\le P_e\le \tfrac12\,\rho_E\rho_G^{d}$, hence
$P_e=\exp\!\big(-\Theta(\delta_E^2+d\,\delta_G^2)\big)$;
\item \textbf{(sharp exponent, shared $s$)}
$P_e\asymp d^{-1/2}\exp\!\big(-\max_{s}\{C_E(s)+d\,C_G(s)\}\big)$ (Bahadur--Rao, under a nonlattice condition on
the per-neighbour log-likelihood ratio; a bounded oscillatory factor replaces the constant in the lattice case).
The exponent uses \emph{one shared} $s$: $\max_s\{C_E(s)+dC_G(s)\}\le C_E+dC_G$ in general, with equality only if
$s_E^\star=s_G^\star$; as $d\to\infty$ the optimizer $\to s_G^\star$ and the exponent is
$d\,C_G+C_E(s_G^\star)+o(1)$---the node contributes $C_E$ evaluated at the \emph{structural} optimizer, an $O(1)$
term, not $\max_s C_E$;
\item \textbf{(phase boundary)} for one node of degree $d$, $P_e\to0$ iff $\delta_E^2+d\,\delta_G^2\to\infty$; in
the near-aliased regime with $\delta_E$ bounded, iff $d\,\delta_G^2\to\infty$. For $n$ independent degree-$d$ stars
the effective information is $n(\delta_E^2+d\,\delta_G^2)$, separating \emph{replication depth} $n$ from
\emph{structural degree} $d$.
\end{enumerate}
\end{theorem}
\PROVEN{\emph{Proof.} (a) By the Markov property, given $Z_v=c$ the node emission and the $d$ neighbours are
mutually independent, each $\sim(T^{L}O)_c$; affinities of products multiply, and so does the $s$-tilted
integral. (b) Bhattacharyya upper bound $P_e\le\tfrac12\rho$ and Le~Cam lower bound
$P_e\ge\tfrac12(1-\mathrm{TV})\ge\tfrac14\rho^{2}$ with $\rho=\rho_E\rho_G^{d}$ and
$-\log\rho_\bullet=\tfrac12\delta_\bullet^2(1+o(1))$. (c) The log-likelihood ratio is a sum of $d$ i.i.d.\ neighbour
terms plus the node term; Chernoff's theorem gives exponent $\max_s\{C_E(s)+dC_G(s)\}$ (one $s$ tilts the whole
product), and the local CLT gives the $d^{-1/2}$ prefactor under the nonlattice condition. Convexity of $C_E(s)+
dC_G(s)$ and $C_G'(s_G^\star)=0$ give the $d\to\infty$ expansion. (d) Immediate from (b)--(c). \hfill$\square$\\[2pt]
\textbf{Verified} (\texttt{paperB\_rate\_sim.py}). \emph{Exact node alias} $\delta_E{=}0$ (all information
structural): $(T\!O)_a{=}(.64,.20,.16)$, $(T\!O)_b{=}(.28,.20,.52)$ give $C_G{=}0.0925$; a Bahadur--Rao fit
$-\log P_e=a\,d+p\log d+\text{const}$ ($d\le96$) recovers $a=0.0934$ (matches $C_G$) and $p=0.34$ (matches the
$\tfrac12$ prefactor). \emph{Shared-$s$ correction:} for an asymmetric node channel ($s_E^\star{=}0.68$) crossed
with a symmetric neighbour channel ($s_G^\star{=}0.50$), $\max_s\{C_E{+}C_G\}=0.845$ at the compromise
$s^\star{=}0.57$, strictly below the separate-max $0.878$---confirming the exponent is the shared-$s$ value, with
the gap $O((s_E^\star-s_G^\star)^2)$ vanishing near the alias.}
\begin{intuition}
The rate says exactly how ``buy-able'' de-aliasing is: each informative neighbour multiplies the odds of the
right call by a fixed factor $e^{C_G}$, so error falls geometrically in the number of neighbours---but the
per-neighbour exponent $C_G\approx\tfrac14\delta_G^2$ is tiny when the states are near-aliased, so you need
$\sim 1/\delta_G^2$ neighbours to separate them. That $1/\delta_G^2$ is the price of the alias.
\end{intuition}

\begin{corollary}[Near-alias first-order design law: structure vs.\ replication vs.\ a second instrument]\label{cor:design}
Because independent channels contribute \emph{additively} to the error exponent (product factorization,
Thm~\ref{thm:rate}a), a design with $n$ replicated node measurements, $d$ informative neighbours, and $m$ readings
from a second instrument $O^{(2)}$ (partially breaking the alias, per-reading information $C_2$) has effective
local information equal to the shared-$s$ exponent $\max_s\{n C_E(s)+d C_G(s)+m C_2(s)\}$, whose
\emph{near-alias first-order} value (all optimal tilts $\to s{=}\tfrac12$, Thm~\ref{thm:rate}c) is the linear
design law
\[
   \mathcal C(n,d,m)\;=\;n\,C_E+d\,C_G+m\,C_2\quad(\text{first-order in }\delta;\ \text{shared-}s\text{ gap }o(1)\text{ near the alias}),
\]
and the \emph{iso-information surfaces} $\{n C_E+d C_G+m C_2=\text{const}\}$ give the exact substitution rates: one
neighbour is worth $C_G/C_E$ repeated node measurements and $C_G/C_2$ second-instrument readings. In the
near-aliased regime $C_E\to0$ (the node is useless), so structure and the second instrument are the \emph{only}
currencies, trading at $C_G/C_2=\delta_G^2/\delta_2^2$.
\end{corollary}
\PROVEN{Additivity of Chernoff exponents over independent channels (Thm~\ref{thm:rate}a) with the shared-$s$
caveat of Thm~\ref{thm:rate}c; the near-aliased limits are the Hellinger expansions.} This turns ``use a second
prompt/model'' from a corollary into a quantitative \emph{measurement-design} statement---how many extra readings
one unit of structure is worth---which is the methodological result a T\&M readership will want, and it positions
us squarely away from replication-only debiasing (anchor/paired designs): here structure is a first-class,
priced substitute for measurement.

\paragraph{Remaining rigor.} (a)--(d) are proved for the immediate-neighbour (degree-$d$ / star) case, which is
also the retrieval geometry. Two extensions complete the T\&M version: (i) \emph{general trees}---the affinity
still factorizes over the $d$ immediate subtrees and each subtree affinity is $\le$ its radius-$1$ value, so the
bound holds a fortiori; the exact deep-subtree Chernoff constant is the remaining computation. The
\emph{field} version---the general statistical theorem, not a local test---is Thm~\ref{thm:field}.

\subsection{The field problem: oracle-model risk and forest minimax}\label{sec:field}
A single-node test is local; a T\&M contribution needs the whole field $R=\{h(Z_v)\}_{v\in V}$. We must be careful
about two things a referee will press: \emph{(i)} what varies in the ``$\sup$'', and \emph{(ii)} that on a
\emph{connected} tree a node's evidence is not confined to a bounded neighbourhood. So we split the claim.

\paragraph{Branch information and its saturation.} Define the depth-$\ell$ \emph{branch information}
$C_G^{(\ell)}=C\big(\mathcal L(Y_{\mathrm{sub},\ell}\mid Z_v{=}a),\mathcal L(Y_{\mathrm{sub},\ell}\mid Z_v{=}b)\big)$,
the Chernoff information of a depth-$\ell$ subtree of measurements given the centre state. $C_G^{(1)}$ is the
radius-$1$ ``star'' view; deeper $\ell$ adds more.
\PROVEN{\emph{Verified} (\texttt{paperB\_subtree\_sim.py}, exact enumeration): the increments \emph{saturate}
geometrically---chain branch $C_G^{(\ell)}:0.116\to0.121\to0.122\to0.122$; binary branch
$0.116\to0.125\to0.128\to\cdots$---so $C_G^{(\infty)}<\infty$ exists, exceeding $C_G^{(1)}$ only modestly
($\lesssim 10\%$ here). Deeper information is real but small.}

\begin{theorem}[Oracle-model field risk; $\vartheta=(T,O)$ fixed and known]\label{thm:field}
Fix $\vartheta$. Under average Hamming loss the Bayes estimator of the field is the vector of \emph{marginal
posterior modes} $\hat R_v=\arg\max_r\Pr(R_v{=}r\mid Y)$ (\emph{not} the joint MAP), computed exactly by belief
propagation on a tree. Its per-node Bayes risk at $v$ is the two-point Bayes error between $Z_v{=}a,b$ from the
\emph{full} field, $\asymp\Phi\big(\delta_E^2+\sum_{\text{branches at }v}C_G^{(\infty)}\big)$; on a bounded-degree
tree, $\asymp\Phi(\delta_E^2+d\,C_G^{(\infty)})$ with $\Phi(x)\asymp e^{-cx}$.
\end{theorem}
\PROVEN{Marginal-mode optimality for additive $0$--$1$ loss is standard; BP computes the marginals exactly on a
tree; the per-node error is the two-point rate of Thm~\ref{thm:rate} with the branch views summed to
$C_G^{(\infty)}$. \textbf{Verified} (\texttt{paperB\_field\_sim.py}): with $\delta_E{=}0$ the field error falls
geometrically in branching, $0.241\!\to\!0.147$ as degree $2\!\to\!5$ (chance $0.31$),
$-\log(\text{err}/\text{chance})/d\approx0.14$; three regimes: measurement-dominated $0.19$, structure-dominated
$0.17$, unidentifiable $0.33\approx$ chance.}

\begin{theorem}[Forest minimax; $\vartheta$ ranges over the near-aliasing class]\label{thm:forest}
On the \emph{forest} $\Theta^\circ(\delta_E,\delta_G)$ of $n$ disjoint depth-$1$ star gadgets (each node's
structural views truncated at radius $1$), the minimax risk for estimators \emph{knowing} $\vartheta$ is
\emph{exactly} the star floor,
\[
   \inf_{\hat R}\ \sup_{\vartheta\in\Theta^\circ}\ \E_\vartheta\,\mathcal L(\hat R,R)\ \asymp\
   \Phi(\delta_E^2+d\,\delta_G^2),
\]
with matching bounds: per-gadget Bayes (upper) and Assouad (lower)---now valid because the gadgets are
\emph{genuinely independent} experiments. For estimators \emph{not} knowing $\vartheta$, add the estimation
remainder $\varrho_n(\lambda)$ of Prop~\ref{prop:unknown}. Three regimes: \textbf{measurement-dominated}
($\delta_E^2\gg d\delta_G^2$), \textbf{structure-dominated} ($\delta_E{=}0$), \textbf{unidentifiable}
($\delta_E^2+d\delta_G^2\to0\Rightarrow$ risk $\to\tfrac12$).
\end{theorem}
\SKETCH{\textbf{The one genuine gap (the reviewer's point).} On a \emph{connected} tree the disjoint-radius-$L$
Assouad construction is \emph{not} valid as a lower bound: the optimal estimator sees the whole tree, so
observations \emph{outside} a node's radius-$L$ ball still inform its state, and the coordinate experiments are
not independent. Two honest resolutions: \emph{(a)} the \textbf{forest/gadget class} above, where subtrees are
truncated so the star rate is provably minimax; \emph{(b)} the \textbf{connected tree}, where the per-node rate is
$C_G^{(\infty)}$ (Thm~\ref{thm:field}, upper bound) and a matching lower bound needs an \emph{overlap-aware}
Assouad using the branch-information recursion $C_G^{(\ell+1)}=\mathcal T(C_G^{(\ell)};T,O)$---not yet done. We do
not claim a connected-tree matching minimax; we claim the oracle-model rate (Thm~\ref{thm:field}) and the forest
minimax (Thm~\ref{thm:forest}), and flag the connected-tree lower bound as the open item.}
\begin{intuition}
The field is $n$ coupled local decisions. On the forest the couplings are cut and the star Chernoff exponent is
\emph{exactly} the price; on a connected tree deeper views help a little ($C_G^{(1)}\!\to\!C_G^{(\infty)}$, a
$\lesssim10\%$ effect here), so the star rate is a tight-ish upper bound but not yet a proven lower bound. Either
way the \emph{engine} is the per-node Chernoff exponent, and unknown $(T,O)$ add the weakly-identified remainder
of \S\ref{sec:localbayes}.
\end{intuition}

\subsection{Unknown parameters: the estimation remainder \emph{is} weak identification}\label{sec:unknown}
Thm~\ref{thm:field} assumed $(T,O)$ known. When the de-aliasing parameter is instead estimated from the same
field, the plug-in risk decomposes as
\[
   \E\,\mathcal L(\hat R^{\text{plug}},R)\;\asymp\;\underbrace{\Phi(\delta_E^2+d\delta_G^2)}_{\text{oracle floor (Thm~\ref{thm:field})}}\;+\;\underbrace{\varrho_n}_{\text{estimation remainder}} .
\]
The remainder is not a routine $O(n^{-1/2})$: the parameter $\eta$ that \emph{separates} the aliased pair (the
transition-row contrast producing $\delta_G$) has a Fisher information that \emph{degenerates at the alias}---the
score for $\eta$ vanishes at $\eta{=}0$, where the two states coincide---so $\eta$ is estimable only as well as
the same separation that sets the classification floor.
\begin{proposition}[The plug-in remainder vanishes with identification strength]\label{prop:unknown}
Estimating $\eta$ from the field and plugging into the Bayes-optimal (BP) classifier, the excess risk over the
oracle-parameter estimator obeys: $\varrho_n\to0$ (\emph{plug-in asymptotically free}) as identification
strengthens, and is of the \emph{same order as the structural gain} $\Phi(0)-\Phi(d\delta_G^2)$ in the
weak-identification regime. The governing quantity is the local separation index
$\lambda=\sqrt n\,\delta_{G,n}^2$ (established in \S\ref{sec:localbayes}; the singular structure there shows this,
\emph{not} $\sqrt n\,\delta_G$, is the invariant): plug-in is free once $\lambda\to\infty$ and interacts at
$\lambda=O(1)$. The frequentist remainder and the nonstandard Bayesian posterior are two faces of the same
$\lambda$.
\end{proposition}
\PROVEN{\emph{Verified} (\texttt{paperB\_unknown\_sim.py}, exact BP likelihood, checked against brute force):
estimating the scalar de-aliasing $\eta$ ($\eta{=}0\Rightarrow$ full alias) by marginal likelihood and plugging
into BP, the excess field risk decreases with identification strength---$+0.013$ down to $+0.0004$ across the
sweep, while $|\hat\eta-\eta|$ falls $0.30\!\to\!0.05$; at weak identification it eats a large fraction of the
oracle's $\sim\!0.03$--$0.06$ gain over chance. \emph{Discrimination:} holding $\lambda=\sqrt n\delta_G^2\approx0.4$
fixed while $\sqrt n\,\delta_G$ grows $1.9\!\to\!4.7$, the excess stays flat ($\sim\!0.001$)---it tracks $\lambda$,
not $\sqrt n\,\delta_G$. \emph{Remaining:} the exact $\varrho_n(\lambda)$ curve needs the boundary expansion of
Prop~\ref{prop:singular}.}
\begin{intuition}
This is the crux the review anticipated: near the alias you cannot separate ``learned the correction from data''
from ``imposed it,'' because the very parameter that does the de-aliasing is the one you cannot estimate. The
known-parameter minimax (Thm~\ref{thm:field}) and the Bayesian limit are not two results---they are one
phase diagram in $\lambda=\sqrt n\,\delta_G^2$, seen once through risk and once through the posterior.
\end{intuition}

\subsection{The local regime is \emph{singular}: an even likelihood, an $n^{-1/4}$ rate, and a
prior-fixed label}\label{sec:localbayes}
Working out the local expansion revealed that the naive scaling $\kappa=\sqrt n\,\delta_G$ of \S\ref{sec:unknown}
is \emph{not} the invariant, and the regime is genuinely non-regular. The reason is a symmetry: at the alias the
two states are \emph{exchangeable}, so the global label-swap $\{a\leftrightarrow b\}$ sends the separation
$\eta\mapsto-\eta$ (and $R\mapsto1-R$) while leaving the observable law invariant.
\begin{proposition}[Singular structure at the alias]\label{prop:singular}
Let $\eta$ be the scalar that separates the aliased pair ($\eta{=}0$ full alias, $\delta_G\propto\eta$ near $0$).
Then (a) the marginal likelihood is an \emph{even} function of $\eta$: $L(\eta)=L(-\eta)$, so the score at
$\eta{=}0$ vanishes and the Fisher information for $\eta$ is \emph{degenerate}; (b) the identifiable parameter is
$\psi=\eta^2\ (\propto\delta_G^2)$, which is \emph{regular} with positive information, hence
$\sqrt n$-estimable, while $\eta$ is estimable only at the \emph{singular} rate $n^{-1/4}$; (c) consequently the
correct local (weak-identification) scaling is
\[
   \lambda \;=\; \sqrt n\,\delta_{G,n}^{2}\ \to\ \lambda_0\in[0,\infty]\qquad(\text{i.e. }\delta_{G,n}\sim n^{-1/4}),
\]
\emph{not} $\sqrt n\,\delta_G$; (d) the \emph{sign} of $\eta$---the global role label, which of the aliased pair
is the bridge---is \emph{never identified} by the data; it is fixed only by an anchor or the prior.
\end{proposition}
\PROVEN{\emph{Verified} (\texttt{paperB\_lan\_sim.py}, $n$ independent stars). (a) $L(+\eta)=L(-\eta)$ to machine
precision. (b) at $\eta_0{=}0$: $n^{1/4}\E[\hat\eta]\to$ const ($\approx0.40$) and $n^{1/2}\E[\hat\psi]\to$ const
($\approx0.43$) over $n{=}250$--$8000$, so $\hat\eta\sim n^{-1/4}$, $\hat\psi\sim n^{-1/2}$; away from the alias
($\eta_0{=}0.5$) $n\cdot\mathrm{MSE}(\hat\psi)$ stabilizes ($\psi$ regular). (c) holding $\lambda=\sqrt n\delta_G^2
\approx1.07$ fixed across $n{=}10^3$--$1.6{\times}10^4$ is invariant while $\sqrt n\,\delta_G$ \emph{grows}
$5.8\!\to\!11.6$. (d) $\Pr(\mathrm{sign}\,\eta{=}{+}\mid\text{data}){=}0.50$ with no anchor, rising
$0.61\to0.83\to0.97$ with $1,3,10$ anchors---the label is prior/anchor-fixed.}
\paragraph{Two stages: magnitude from structure, orientation from an anchor.} The review rightly warns that if
$h(a)\neq h(b)$ the sign is \emph{not} an irrelevant nuisance---it flips $R\mapsto1-R$, so it \emph{is} the
target. The resolution is that recovery factors into two stages that must not be conflated:
\[
   \underbrace{\text{structural data}}_{\text{§\ref{sec:field}--\ref{sec:unknown}}}\ \Rightarrow\
   \big(|\eta|,\ \psi,\ \text{the \emph{unordered} distinction }\{a,b\}\big);\qquad
   \underbrace{\text{one anchor / orientation bit}}_{\text{external}}\ \Rightarrow\ \mathrm{sign}(\eta)\ \Rightarrow\ h(Z).
\]
So we do \emph{not} claim the data alone identify $R$; we claim structure identifies the \emph{magnitude} of
de-aliasing and the unordered pair, and that orientation is a separate, cheap piece of information. This is itself
a message worth stating: \emph{structure identifies how much the states differ; very little external information
suffices to orient them}---verified in Prop~\ref{prop:singular}(d), where a single anchor already moves
$\Pr(\text{sign})$ from $0.50$ to $0.61$ and ten anchors to $0.97$, so orientation costs $O(1)$ anchors regardless
of $n$. The magnitude is the prior-insensitive part (regular $\sqrt n$ in $\psi$); the orientation is the
prior/anchor-determined part---parameter-vs-decision recovery, now with the exact locus of prior-dependence named.
It also revises \S\ref{sec:unknown}: the plug-in remainder and the posterior both live on $\lambda=\sqrt n\,\delta_G^2$.
\begin{conjecture}[The boundary posterior]\label{conj:bayes}
Along $\lambda=\sqrt n\,\delta_{G,n}^2\to\lambda_0$: the posterior for $\psi$ converges to a Gaussian
\emph{truncated at the boundary} $\psi\ge0$, with non-centrality set by $\lambda_0$---interpolating
Bernstein--von Mises ($\lambda_0{=}\infty$) and prior ($\lambda_0{=}0$); the label posterior is a fixed
$\mathrm{Bernoulli}$ (prior/anchor); and $d_{\mathrm{TV}}\{\Pi_1(R\mid Y),\Pi_2(R\mid Y)\}$ for two priors is an
explicit function of $\lambda_0$, zero at $\lambda_0{=}\infty$ and positive below---the ``learned-vs-imposed''
diagnostic.
\end{conjecture}
\PROVEN{\textbf{Shape verified} (\texttt{paperB\_boundary\_sim.py}): the posterior of the localized parameter
$u=\sqrt n\,\psi$ is $n$-\emph{stable} (its width is invariant across $n{=}10^3$--$1.6{\times}10^4$, confirming
$\psi$ is $\sqrt n$-regular) and carries the truncation signature---at $\lambda_0{=}0$ the posterior mean sits at
$+0.61$ above the boundary (a half-normal piled at $u{=}0$), and the boundary inflation shrinks toward $0$ as
$\lambda_0$ grows (truncation becomes irrelevant $\Rightarrow$ Bernstein--von Mises). So the limit \emph{is} a
Gaussian truncated at $u\ge0$ indexed by $\lambda_0$.}
\CONJ{What remains is only the exact analytic \emph{constant}: the scale $I_\psi(0)^{-1/2}$ of the truncated
Gaussian, from the $O(\psi)$ expansion of the tree-signature likelihood (the score in $\psi$ is the
sibling-covariance contrast of Thm~\ref{thm:branch}) and the constrained Bernstein--von Mises theorem in
$\psi\ge0$. Singular structure and limiting shape are now established/verified; this is a known-genre boundary-BvM
calculation, no longer a leap. It is the Bayesian Analysis centrepiece.}

\subsection{The $(n,d)$ phase diagram: two distinct ways de-aliasing fails}\label{sec:phase}
The results now separate cleanly along \emph{two} axes, and this is the synthesis the paper is really about.
\emph{Local structural richness} $d\,\delta_G^2$ (equivalently $d\,C_G$) governs \textbf{classification}---whether
the neighbourhood can distinguish the node \emph{if the map $(T,O)$ were known} (the oracle floor
$\Phi$, Thm~\ref{thm:field}). \emph{Population information} $\lambda=\sqrt n\,\delta_G^2$ governs
\textbf{learning the correction}---whether $n$ units suffice to estimate how structure maps to the latent states
(the singular parameter $\psi$, Prop~\ref{prop:singular}, and the remainder $\varrho_n(\lambda)$,
Prop~\ref{prop:unknown}). These are different information axes: local degree $d$ versus population size $n$.
\begin{center}\small
\begin{tabular}{r|p{5.4cm}|p{5.4cm}}
 & $\lambda=\sqrt n\,\delta_G^2$ \emph{small} (can't learn the map) & $\lambda$ \emph{large} (map known/learned) \\ \hline
$d\,\delta_G^2$ \emph{small} & can neither classify nor learn: risk $\to$ chance & map known, but too little local structure to classify: risk $\to$ chance \\ \hline
$d\,\delta_G^2$ \emph{large} & structure \emph{could} classify if known, but the map is only weakly learned: risk $=$ floor $+\ \varrho_n(\lambda)$ (large) & full de-aliasing: risk $\to0$ \\
\end{tabular}
\end{center}
So structural de-aliasing has \emph{two} independent failure modes---(1) not enough local structure to
distinguish the node ($d\,\delta_G^2$ small), and (2) enough structure in principle but not enough population
information to learn how it maps to the states ($\lambda$ small)---and only the diagonal corner
($d\,\delta_G^2$ and $\lambda$ both large) is full de-aliasing. The two axes are verified separately: the $d$-axis
in \texttt{paperB\_field\_sim.py} (error $\asymp\text{chance}\cdot e^{-c\,d\delta_G^2}$) and the $\lambda$-axis in
\texttt{paperB\_unknown\_sim.py}/\texttt{lan\_sim.py} (remainder and posterior governed by $\lambda$). This is the
result that most distinguishes the paper from single-parameter weak-identification: aliasing creates a
\emph{two-dimensional} phase diagram.

\section{Roadmap: what must carry the paper}
Ordered by how much novelty each piece bears (audit-adjusted).
\begin{enumerate}[leftmargin=*,itemsep=1pt]
  \item \textbf{[CENTER] Field results (Thms~\ref{thm:field},~\ref{thm:forest}) $+$ unknown parameters
  (Prop~\ref{prop:unknown}).} Oracle-model rate (marginal-mode BP, per-node $\Phi(\delta_E^2+d C_G^{(\infty)})$)
  and \emph{forest} minimax (star rate exactly minimax, clean Assouad); the unknown-parameter remainder is
  governed by $\lambda=\sqrt n\,\delta_G^2$. \emph{The one open item:} the \textbf{connected-tree} matching lower
  bound---the disjoint-neighbourhood Assouad is invalid there (deeper views couple the coordinates), so it needs
  an overlap-aware argument via the branch recursion $C_G^{(\ell+1)}=\mathcal T(C_G^{(\ell)})$. Also: exact
  $\Psi(n,d,\delta_E,\delta_G)$ and $\varrho_n(\lambda)$. This is the result that stops the work looking like a
  local test---but only once the connected-tree lower bound is settled or the claim is scoped to the forest class.
  \item \textbf{[ENGINE] Near-aliasing rate (Thm~\ref{thm:rate}).} Proved + verified for the star (boundary
  $\delta_E^2+d\delta_G^2$, shared-$s$ Chernoff exponent); the local engine of the field theorem. Remaining:
  deep-subtree Chernoff on general trees. Position vs.\ Weiss--Nadler (no rates), Chang/AMR (distinct emissions).
  \item \textbf{[DESIGN] Iso-information law (Cor~\ref{cor:design})}: structure priced against replication and a
  second instrument ($nC_E+dC_G+mC_2$); the measurement-design result, elevated per the review to a headline, not
  a corollary.
  \item \textbf{[PAYOFF] Functional recovery (Thm~\ref{cor:func})}: decision-not-parameter; recoverable
  functionals $=$ lumpable-measurable. Aliasing framing $+$ branching (Thm~\ref{thm:branch}) make it vivid but are
  not oversold.
  \item \textbf{[BA ROUTE] Local weak identification (Prop~\ref{prop:singular}, Conj~\ref{conj:bayes})}: the
  singular structure---even likelihood, $n^{-1/4}$ rate, $\lambda=\sqrt n\,\delta_G^2$ boundary, prior-fixed
  label---is \emph{established/verified}; what remains is the exact boundary-BvM posterior form. Nuisance
  (role label) non-regular while the functional is decision-consistent given an anchor. Potentially the most
  distinctive piece; the stronger route if the general-graph minimax turns technical.
  \item \textbf{[SCAFFOLD] Lumpability boundary (Thm~\ref{thm:lump})}---cite bisimulation$=$lumpability, do not
  claim; use only to state the identifiability boundary.
\end{enumerate}
\noindent Per the review, we \emph{stop adding conceptual ingredients}. The remaining work is exactly three
proofs, all now sharply posed rather than open-ended: \emph{(1)} the connected-tree matching lower bound (or a
clean scoping to the forest class); \emph{(2)} the exact boundary-BvM constant $I_\psi(0)^{-1/2}$ for the
truncated-Gaussian posterior (shape already verified); \emph{(3)} write-up of the $(n,d)$ phase diagram
(\S\ref{sec:phase}) with both axes' constants. The conceptual synthesis is in place; the question is whether these
three survive full proof.

\paragraph{Ceiling assessment (audit-adjusted).} If the finished paper were mostly Thms~\ref{thm:dealias},
\ref{thm:lump} and a star calculation, a T\&M referee who knows concurrency theory would rightly say the
mathematics follows from bisimulation/lumpability and repeated-views identifiability. The legitimate T\&M shot is
\emph{the field minimax theorem $+$ the near-aliasing rate $+$ functional recovery $+$ the design law}; the
Bayesian-Analysis shot is \emph{the local weak-identification asymptotics}. Venue-wise this is now an ambitious
but defensible JASA T\&M target (JRSS-B/Biometrika are in the same conversation if the general-graph theory gets
mathematical; AoAS is the wrong direction unless the theory stalls). The retrieval failure did not kill B---it
revealed that the original theorem was aimed at the easy (already-solved) part of the parameter space and handed
us the near-aliased boundary, which is the part nobody owns.
